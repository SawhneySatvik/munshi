# AI-Era Solo Founders (2022–2026) Who Reached $1M+ ARR, Annual Revenue, or a $1M+ Exit

Research date: 13 Nov 2026. Method note: WebFetch to several primary hosts (saastr.com, moneywise.com, dev.to, trustmrr.com) was blocked by the session's network policy, so every finding below comes from search-engine results and their snippets/summaries, not full-page reads. Live TrustMRR pages could not be opened. Figures are dated wherever the source gives a date; undated figures are flagged. Scope excludes mobile-first apps (Cal AI is covered only to flag that it was co-founded), classic pre-2022 SaaS, and Pieter Levels' pre-AI products.

Evidence-quality codes used throughout:
- **[V]** Verified by a payment processor (TrustMRR, Stripe-connected, or a Stripe-published customer story)
- **[A]** Acquisition announcement or investor/public-company disclosure
- **[P-doc]** Press that says it saw financial documents (e.g., NYT for Medvi)
- **[SR]** Founder self-reported (X posts, X bio, blog, newsletter, screenshots, podcast)
- **[2nd]** Secondary aggregator, listicle or estimate (Latka, case-study blogs). Lowest reliability.

---

## Key Question 1: Who are the most credible solo builders who reached $1M+ with AI-native or AI-built products since 2022, and how fast did they get there compared with pre-AI indie businesses?

### Takeaway
The most credible qualifying cases are Maor Shlomo (Base44: $80M exit roughly 6 months after launch; total payout reported as up to about $280M), Pieter Levels (Photo AI at about $1.2–1.6M annualized), Danny Postma (HeadshotPro, $10M+ lifetime revenue), Tony Dinh (TypingMind, $1M trailing-12-month revenue in 20 months), Marc Lou ($1.03M in 2025), Nevo David (Postiz, TrustMRR-verified $250K+ MRR in Oct 2026), and Yasser Elsaid (Chatbase, $1M ARR in 117 days; solo only at the start). Medvi is the largest by far ($401M in 2025 sales), but it is a two-person company built on outsourced clinical partners, not a solo software app. The fastest AI-era solo cases reached $1M run-rates in weeks to months (17 days, 117 days, under 3 months, about 5 months). Stripe's cohort median for top AI startups is about 11 months, and for SaaS about 15 months. Pre-AI indie SaaS such as Bannerbear took years.

#### Profile 1: Maor Shlomo, Base44 (Israel). The cleanest AI-era solo exit
- **What it does:** A natural-language "vibe coding" builder that turns prompts into full-stack apps.
- **Launch:** January 2025, started as a side project — [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3393744947939456); [What a Startup](https://whatastartup.substack.com/p/a-solo-founder-just-sold-his-6-months-old-ai-startup-for-80-million-dollars)
- **Founder background:** Not a first-time founder. He co-founded big-data company Explorium in 2017 and was its CEO; Explorium raised about $125M (another source says about $130M) — [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/s1iflnlelx). Possible credibility and network advantages follow from this.
- **Traction:** 10,000 users within 3 weeks of launch [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3393744947939456). About 250,000 users at acquisition [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/saastr-ai-app-of-the-week-base44-one-founder-zero-funding-80-million-from-wix-in-6-months-now-at-200-million-arr/).
- **Profit:** Shlomo posted on X and LinkedIn that Base44 made **$189,000 profit in May 2025 after LLM token costs**, about double his $100K forecast [SR] — [Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2025/06/19/wix-acquires-ai-coding-startup-base44-for-80m/)
- **ARR at sale:** About **$3.5M ARR** (June 2025) per [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/saastr-ai-app-of-the-week-base44-one-founder-zero-funding-80-million-from-wix-in-6-months-now-at-200-million-arr/) and [Latka blog](https://getlatka.com/blog/base44-revenue-acquired-wix). CTech said "just a few million" [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/n4je37x0k). [2nd/press estimate]
- **Deal:** Wix paid **$80M up front plus milestone payments through 2029** — [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-wix-acquires-israeli-vibe-coding-co-base44-1001513267). The deal closed 18 June 2025 [A] — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/base44/). It was bootstrapped with no outside funding — [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/wix-buys-ai-startup-base44-for-80m-six-month-sprint-deal)
- **Team (solo caveat):** DesignRush says Wix confirmed **8 employees** sharing **$25M in retention bonuses** [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/wix-buys-ai-startup-base44-for-80m-six-month-sprint-deal). Globes reported **6 employees, all hired in the month before the deal** [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001513267). He was effectively solo for about 5 of the 6 months.
- **Post-acquisition ARR (company-reported via Wix and press):** $59M at end of 2025, over $100M in early March 2026, and **$200M in August 2026** — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/base44/). ARR "doubled to $200M in five months" — [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/fu5xrwt7i)
- **Founder payout:** Reached **about $150M** after a later Wix payout [CTech tag page](https://www.calcalistech.com/tags/Base44/0). The latest CTech estimate of total deal value is **up to about $280M**, including $32M of newly disclosed potential performance payments [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/fu5xrwt7i). These figures are from different dates and are not additive.
- **Role of AI:** The product itself is an AI app generator. I found no source in this session on which AI coding tools he used to build it (see Gaps).

#### Profile 2: Matthew Gallagher, Medvi (Los Angeles). Huge revenue, but it qualifies as "solo" only with caveats
- **What it does:** A direct-to-consumer telehealth marketer for compounded GLP-1 weight-loss drugs. Licensed physicians, prescriptions, pharmacy fulfillment, shipping and compliance are outsourced to **CareValidate and OpenLoop Health** — [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/company/medvi/); [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/robotica-e-ia/nyt-publico-historia-emprendedor-ia-que-factura-1-800-millones-dos-empleados-se-olvido-mencionar-unas-cuantas-cosas)
- **Launch:** About September 2024, with roughly $20K of starting capital. It had 300 customers in month one and about 1,000 more in month two — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/127216-from-20k-to-1-8b-in-revenue-how-one-founder-built-a-telehealth-giant-wit/)
- **Revenue:** **$401M in sales in the first full year (2025)**, 250,000 customers and a 16.2% net margin. **Projected $1.8B for 2026.** The NYT (2 April 2026) was "given access to Medvi's financials to verify its revenue and profits" [P-doc] — [The Star (NYT syndication)](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/04/11/how-ai-helped-1-man-and-his-brother-build-a-us18-billion-company). Profit was about $65M. For comparison, Hims & Hers had $2.4B revenue in 2025 with 2,442 employees — [OnHealthcare.tech](https://www.onhealthcare.tech/p/the-18b-ozempic-middleman-and-what). A conflicting $180M revenue figure appeared on [Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/aidriven-startup-medvi-achieves-180m-revenue-with-just-two-employees-70567); it is probably an earlier or partial figure.
- **Team:** Gallagher plus his only employee, his younger brother Elliot. There are also freelance contractors, account managers and outside agencies — [The Star/NYT](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/04/11/how-ai-helped-1-man-and-his-brother-build-a-us18-billion-company); [GV Wire](https://gvwire.com/2026/04/05/a-1-8-billion-company-with-two-employees-ai-made-it-possible/)
- **Role of AI:** He used AI tools to write the platform's code, write site copy, generate ad images and videos, and run customer service. The support chatbot sometimes **made up drug prices** — [The Star/NYT](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/04/11/how-ai-helped-1-man-and-his-brother-build-a-us18-billion-company); [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/robotica-e-ia/nyt-publico-historia-emprendedor-ia-que-factura-1-800-millones-dos-empleados-se-olvido-mencionar-unas-cuantas-cosas)
- **Distribution:** Paid social and performance ads. Gallagher said "maybe 30%" of advertising ran through affiliates. Active Medvi-related ad campaigns reportedly fell from over 5,000 to about 2,800 after scrutiny — [Pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/healthtech/news/medvis-401-m-revenue-surge-shadowed-by-fake-doctor-ads-probe-9ead623d-defc-4b17-862c-6b8f01739da7). There were allegations of AI-generated "doctors" in ads; the company said it updated its marketing — [Mediaweek](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/ai-could-make-medvi-1b-in-sales-but-it-could-also-break-it)
- **Pricing:** Compounded semaglutide at $179 for the first month, then $299 per refill (billed every 28 days) — [GlobeNewswire release, 20 Dec 2025](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/12/20/3208796/0/en/Is-MEDVi-the-Best-GLP-1-Supplier-for-2026-Platform-Lists-Compounded-Semaglutide-at-179-Month-With-No-Contract-Ahead-of-Weight-Loss-Season.html); [Trimi](https://trytrimi.com/blog/medvi-cost)
- **Regulatory status:** The **FDA sent a warning letter in Feb 2026** for misbranding compounded drugs, before the NYT piece ran — [Drug Discovery Trends](https://www.drugdiscoverytrends.com/the-new-york-times-spotlighted-medvi-the-fda-had-already-warned-the-self-proclaimed-fastest-growing-company-in-history/). Techdirt and Futurism criticized the NYT coverage — [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/company/medvi/); [Pixel Envy linklog of Futurism](https://pxlnv.com/linklog/nyt-medvi/)
- **Does it count?** It is not a software or app product. It has 2 employees plus contractors, and its regulated work is outsourced. It is best treated as the canonical "AI-leveraged tiny team" case, not a solo app.

#### Profile 7: Nevo David (Israel). Postiz. Possibly the largest TrustMRR-verified solo AI-era SaaS

## Key Question 2: How reliable is each revenue claim? (Verified vs. self-reported vs. press estimates)

### Takeaway
Only a handful of these headline numbers are verified by a third party: Base44's deal (public-company acquisition announcement), Medvi's 2025 revenue (NYT reviewed financials), Chatbase's $10M ARR (Stripe-published customer story), and Postiz and Marc Lou's TrustMRR listings (Stripe-connected). Most of the famous indie figures (Photo AI, HeadshotPro's $300K/mo, TypingMind, fly.pieter.com, Musicfy) rest on founder self-reports that aggregator sites copy, often with conflicting numbers and no dates.

## Key Question 3: What distribution channels worked, and how much did an existing audience matter?

### Takeaway
An existing X/Twitter audience was the dominant launch channel for the best-known solo founders: Levels, Marc Lou, Tony Dinh, Danny Postma, Tibo, and Shlomo's build-in-public posts. Durable growth then came from channels that compound: SEO and affiliates (HeadshotPro), open-source/GitHub as a free tier plus affiliates (Postiz), B2B team plans (TypingMind), and paid performance ads with affiliates (Medvi). Chatbase is the counterexample: it launched to 16 followers and rode the ChatGPT wave.

### Cited Findings
- **Chatbase:** Launched to **16 Twitter followers**; growth came from the product and the timing of the ChatGPT wave, not audience — [Solo Founders newsletter](https://solofounders.beehiiv.com/p/9m-arr-no-vc-solo-founder)

- **TypingMind:** Shifted toward recurring B2B Team revenue, which made up more than 50% of revenue by late 2025 — [Nomadpreneur](https://nomadpreneur.beehiiv.com/p/solo-founder-who-turned-100-into-1-500-000-no-team-no-investors)
- **Cal AI (co-founded, mobile):** Influencer and short-form video distribution is widely cited; it is covered by another researcher — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/09/27/gen-z-founder-treats-college-like-vacation-30-million-app-by-18/)
- **Lovable:** Reached $400M ARR in 14 months with almost no paid ads, mainly through community and user showcase stories — [The AI Corner](https://www.the-ai-corner.com/i/199297364)

### Inferences
- Audience mattered most for launch spikes and for monetizing non-recurring products: fly.pieter.com ads, ShipFast and TrustMRR. When the novelty faded, audience alone did not hold revenue, as the ShipFast and fly.pieter.com declines show.
- The channels that held up longer were the ones that compound regardless of founder fame: SEO, affiliates, open-source repos and B2B seat expansion.
- I found no strong evidence that Product Hunt was decisive for any $1M+ case in this set.

### Gaps
- I found no quantified channel breakdowns (for example, % from SEO versus X) for Photo AI, TypingMind or Base44.
- Musicfy's distribution was not confirmed (presumably viral AI covers on TikTok/YouTube, but unsourced).

## Key Question 4: How durable are these businesses (churn, competition from foundation-model providers, wrapper risk, API costs/margins)?

### Takeaway
Durability is mixed and often weak. Several flagship solo AI-era products have shrunk sharply from their peaks: ShipFast is down about 98%, fly.pieter.com went to about $0, and Photo AI fell from about $138K to about $100K/mo self-reported. Those that held up either moved upmarket into B2B or teams (TypingMind, Chatbase), repositioned for AI agents (Postiz), or sold at the peak (Base44, Cal AI). Margins can be high (Photo AI about 76% profit; Base44 profitable after token costs), but regulatory and IP risk can be existential (Medvi FDA warning, Musicfy label claims).

### Cited Findings
- **ShipFast:** About $126K/mo (2024 peak), then about $25K (2025), then about $2K (2026) — [aiidelist](https://aiidelist.com/blog/shipfast-revenue-collapse-marc-lou-2026). Marc Lou's 2025 total was 20% below 2024 — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/what-marc-lou-s-1m-year-reveals-about-solo-saas-compounding-Kd7SbxGXTYn5gMdfoY8R)
- **fly.pieter.com:** From a "$1M ARR" claim (Mar 2025) to a reported "$0/m" in his X bio (mid-2026) — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/promptway/he-built-a-flight-simulator-in-three-hours-and-hit-1m-a-year-in-17-days-then-it-went-to-zero-1b1l)
- **Photo AI:** $132–138K MRR (late 2025) fell to a self-reported $100–105K/mo in 2026. Profit was $80K on $105K revenue (6 Mar 2026), about a 76% margin — [Photo AI teardown](https://td-site-dx7.pages.dev/case-studies/photo-ai/)
- **Base44 margins:** Profitable at $189K in May 2025 even after LLM token costs — [Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2025/06/19/wix-acquires-ai-coding-startup-base44-for-80m/)
- **TypingMind:** The BYO-API-key model means users pay token costs directly, which removes inference cost from the founder — [TypingMind buy](https://www.typingmind.com/buy); [Zemith](https://www.zemith.com/en/learn/typingmind-pricing). One unverified newsletter claims about 90% margin — [Nomadpreneur](https://nomadpreneur.beehiiv.com/p/solo-founder-who-turned-100-into-1-500-000-no-team-no-investors). By late 2025 more than 50% of revenue was B2B recurring.
- **Chatbase:** Positions itself as a profitable challenger to Sierra, Decagon and Intercom Fin, meaning well-funded competitors, at $10M ARR with a team — [WBOC press release](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/press_releases/chatbase-reaches-10m-arr-as-a-profitable-challenger-to-sierra-decagon-and-fin/article_ea903107-b04c-55a9-a12d-810797b1f35b.html)
- **HeadshotPro:** Cut the number of headshots per price tier since Nov 2024 ($39 bought 100 headshots in Nov 2024 versus 50 now) — [Techpresso](https://academy.techpresso.co/reviews/is-headshotpro-worth-it). The cause (cost or positioning) is not stated.
- **Medvi:** FDA warning letter (Feb 2026) and ad-practice scrutiny; Mediaweek says FDA scrutiny "threatens to undermine the company's entire business model" — [Drug Discovery Trends](https://www.drugdiscoverytrends.com/the-new-york-times-spotlighted-medvi-the-fda-had-already-warned-the-self-proclaimed-fastest-growing-company-in-history/); [Mediaweek](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/ai-could-make-medvi-1b-in-sales-but-it-could-also-break-it). Net margin was 16.2% (2025) — [OnHealthcare.tech](https://www.onhealthcare.tech/p/the-18b-ozempic-middleman-and-what)
- **Musicfy:** Received copyright claims from Sony and Warner — [Partnerkin](https://partnerkin.com/en/blog/case_study/arib-khan-musicfy)
- **Feature commoditization:** "Competing on features is over, because anyone can build any feature in a weekend now" — Jon Yongfook — [Bannerbear blog](https://www.bannerbear.com/blog/how-to-pivot-your-saas-company-in-the-age-of-ai/)
- **Postiz:** Repositioned for AI agents; growth went from about $21K to $145K MRR in 4 months (2026) — [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/postiz)
- **Tibo:** Says he regrets selling earlier products for $8M — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/heres-how-tibo-sold-two-products-for-8m-and-why-he-ll-never-do-it-again-iwykhfENoZqJgRLU33zv)
- **Lovable:** "The search for the first billion-dollar vibe-coded company continues." Contrary questions whether vibe-coded products can survive production realities — [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/report/can-vibes-survive-contact-with-reality)

### Inferences
- **Wrapper risk shows up in the data.** Thin consumer image wrappers (Photo AI) and dev boilerplates (ShipFast) declined as foundation-model image tools and AI coding agents commoditized their value. The cause is my inference; the sources document the declines, not their causes.
- **Selling at or near the peak** (Base44 after about 6 months; Cal AI after about 22 months) turned volatile AI-era revenue into durable founder wealth. Base44's founder may have been the biggest winner precisely because he exited before the commoditization curve hit.
- **Gross margins are high when** users bring their own keys (TypingMind) or pricing sits well above per-generation cost (Photo AI, HeadshotPro one-time packs). Margins are thinner for heavy-inference builders, though Base44 was still profitable.
- **The most durable solo cases moved toward B2B or team plans, open-source moats, or agent-native positioning,** away from purely consumer wrappers.

### Gaps
- I found no churn or retention data for any profiled product.
- There is no direct evidence of specific foundation-model launches (for example ChatGPT image generation or Google image models) causing specific revenue drops; this is inferred only.
- Per-unit API costs for Photo AI and HeadshotPro were not found.

## Key Question 5: What is the state of the "one-person billion-dollar company" discourse as of 2026, and are there credible cases approaching it?

### Takeaway
Sam Altman's prediction (a group-chat betting pool among tech CEOs on the year of the first one-person $1B company) became a mainstream talking point. In April 2026, the NYT profile of Medvi set off claims that "it's here," including a Marginal Revolution post titled "Sam Altman's prediction has come through." Strictly, no case qualifies. Medvi has 2 employees plus contractors and outsourced clinical partners, no valuation, and "$1.8B" is projected 2026 revenue. Base44 is the strongest solo-founder-to-large-outcome case, but the founder's payout (up to about $280M) is well short of $1B, and the $200M ARR was built under Wix with a team.

### Cited Findings
- **The prediction:** Altman reportedly said he and tech CEO friends have a betting pool on when the first one-person billion-dollar company will appear. Sources disagree on whether he said it in 2023 or 2024 — [Founder Institute](https://fi.co/insight/the-one-person-unicorn-how-solo-founders-are-building-billion-dollar-companies-with-ai-in-2026); [China Times (2024 prediction)](https://wantrich.chinatimes.com/news/20260403900241-420501)
- **Medvi as the "arrival":** A Marginal Revolution post was titled "Sam Altman's prediction has come through" (Apr 2026) — [Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/04/sam-altmans-prediction-has-come-through.html). PYMNTS ran "The One-Person Billion-Dollar Company Is Here" — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=3623393). GV Wire ran "A $1.8 billion company with two employees" — [GV Wire](https://gvwire.com/2026/04/05/a-1-8-billion-company-with-two-employees-ai-made-it-possible/)
- **Objections:**
  - Medvi has 2 employees plus contractors, has raised no outside funding and has **no official valuation** — [GV Wire](https://gvwire.com/2026/04/05/a-1-8-billion-company-with-two-employees-ai-made-it-possible/)
  - "Billion dollars of what?" — [Investing 101](https://investing101.substack.com/p/the-1b-rorschach-test)
  - "The NYT got played by a telehealth scam" — [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/company/medvi/)
  - The real work sits with partners CareValidate and OpenLoop — [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/robotica-e-ia/nyt-publico-historia-emprendedor-ia-que-factura-1-800-millones-dos-empleados-se-olvido-mencionar-unas-cuantas-cosas)
  - The idea is "technically possible but practically fragile" — [Soluntech](https://www.soluntech.com/blog/the-myth-and-reality-of-the-one-person-billion-dollar-company/)
- **Contrary Research:** "The search for the first billion-dollar vibe-coded company continues" — [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/report/can-vibes-survive-contact-with-reality)
- **Base44 as the closest software case:** One founder, 6 months, $80M up front, payout reported as up to about $280M total, and product ARR of $200M by Aug 2026 inside Wix — [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/fu5xrwt7i); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/base44/)
- **Tiny-team benchmarks for comparison (not solo):**
  - Lovable added $100M of revenue in one month with 146 employees (Mar 2026) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/11/lovable-says-it-added-100m-in-revenue-last-month-alone-with-just-146-employees/)
  - Cursor was reported at $3B ARR (May 2026) and a $29.3B valuation — [Wikipedia: Cursor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cursor_(company))
  - Cal AI (3–4 co-founders) was sold for a rumored over $100M — [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/cal-ai)
- **Unreliable claims to avoid repeating:** Blog content cites "350 unicorns founded by a single founder" and other unsourced figures — [Founder Institute and similar SEO blogs](https://fi.co/insight/the-one-person-unicorn-how-solo-founders-are-building-billion-dollar-companies-with-ai-in-2026). Note that "single founder" there means one founder with many employees, which is a different concept.

### Inferences
- The discourse has blurred three different things: (a) one founder (with employees), (b) one employee, and (c) $1B in revenue versus $1B in valuation. Medvi fits "tiny team + $1B+ revenue run-rate (projected)." Base44 fits "one founder, AI-native product, large exit," but not $1B.
- If Medvi's 2026 revenue reaches about $1.8B with 2 employees, it is the closest real-world match on revenue per employee. Its durability depends on regulation (compounded GLP-1s, FDA, ad practices) rather than AI.
- No pure-software solo founder in this set is near a $1B valuation as of Nov 2026. The realistic AI-era solo ceiling so far is roughly $1M–$5M ARR kept solo (Levels, Postma, Dinh, David), or a $80M–$280M exit (Shlomo).

### Gaps
- The Altman primary quote, venue and date were not retrieved; sources conflict between 2023 and 2024. The reported "dangerously close" remark (Nov 2025) and Altman's alleged April 2026 statement that "it has arrived" were not verified from primary sources.
- Dario Amodei's reported 2025 prediction (70–80% confidence of a one-person $1B company in 2026) appeared only in unsourced blog content in my searches and was not verified here.
- A Forrester piece titled "Beware The Magical Two-Person, $1 Billion AI-Driven Startup" was referenced in search summaries, but I did not retrieve its URL.
- Medvi's 2026 actual revenue to date (as of Nov 2026) was not found.
