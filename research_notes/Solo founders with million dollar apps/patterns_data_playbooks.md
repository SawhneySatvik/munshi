## Q1. How common is solo founding, and how has it changed through 2026?

### Takeaway
Solo founding has gone from a minority pattern to a large share of new companies. On Carta, solo-founded startups rose from 23.7% (2019) to 36.3% (H1 2025). Stripe Atlas says solo founders were 63% of new C-corps in Q2 2026. Venture capital has not followed: solo founders get a much smaller share of priced-round dollars than their share of company formations.

### Cited Findings
- **Carta, Solo Founders Report 2025 (published Dec 9, 2025):** the share of new startups with a solo founder rose from **23.7% in 2019 to 36.3% in H1 2025**. The dataset is "tens of thousands of U.S. companies" plus founder interviews. — [Carta Solo Founders Report 2025](https://carta.com/data/solo-founders-report); also summarized by [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/solo-founders-rise-from-23-7-to-36-3-ai-tools-enable-the-one-person-startup/)
- **Carta, time to first hire:** median **399 days** for solo founders vs **480 days** for multi-founder companies, so solo founders hire *earlier*. — [Carta Solo Founders Report 2025](https://carta.com/data/solo-founders-report)
- **Carta, funding gap (secondary write-ups of Carta data on 2024-founded startups):** solo-led companies were about **30%** of startups (another write-up says **35%** of 2024 incorporations) but got only **14.7%** of cash raised in priced equity rounds. They were about **17%** of companies that closed a venture round. — [High Street Equity Partners Substack](https://hsep.substack.com/p/solo-founders); [DEV Community / solobillions](https://dev.to/solobillions/the-data-says-solo-founders-are-winning-vcs-havent-caught-up-2imj). Carta's own Founder Ownership Report 2025 says solo founders are "less successful in raising venture capital" than larger teams. — [Carta Founder Ownership Report 2025](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership/)
- **Conflicting Carta framings:** one later write-up says solo founders were about **36% of 2025 startups, up from 31% in 2024**. That does not match the "35% of 2024 incorporations" framing above, probably because the cohorts or measures differ. — [Remarkable Magazine (2026)](https://remarkablemag.com/solo-founder-vs-co-founder-what-the-data-shows/); [Keiretsu Forum NW summary of Carta](https://www.k4northwest.com/articles/the-rise-of-solo-founders-what-cartas-data-reveals-for-entrepreneurs-and-angels) (page could not be fetched)
- **Stripe Atlas, Q2 2026:** solo founders made up **63% of new C-corps** formed through Atlas in Q2 2026, an all-time high. — [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/ai-agents-help-entrepreneurs-run-businesses-alone-3809273); [solofounders.com](https://solofounders.com/blog/solo-founders-are-63-of-new-startups-in-2026-stripe/)
- **Caveat on Stripe's solo classification:** Stripe relies on self-reported employee counts at signup, so founders who hire later can still be counted as "solo." — reported via [TBPN Digest, Aug 3, 2026](https://www.tbpndigest.com/story/2026-08-03/stripe-data-shows-solo-founder-1m-companies-doubling-ai-is-reshaping-the-unit-economics-of-startups) (search summary only)
- **Stripe 2025 annual letter:** in 2025, **20% of Atlas startups charged their first customer within 30 days**, up from **8% in 2020**. — [Stripe 2025 annual letter](https://stripe.com/annual-updates/2025); [Stripe newsroom](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/stripe-2025-update)
- **MicroConf State of Independent SaaS (bootstrapped/indie SaaS population):** a secondary source says **39%** of independent SaaS founders in the 2024 report operate completely solo. A 2025 summary says "about half" of independent SaaS products in MicroConf's survey (~700 founders) are solo-founded. — [Freemius, "Micro-SaaS in 2025"](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/); [MicroConf survey page](https://microconf.com/latest/2021-state-of-independent-saas-survey) (39% not confirmed against the report itself)
- **Tiny Seed (Rob Walling's bootstrapper accelerator):** about **90%** of its bootstrapped portfolio reportedly runs as 1–2 person teams. — [Founder Casebook summary of Walling/MicroConf](https://foundercasebook.com/guides/rob-walling-microconf-bootstrapped-saas) (secondary)

### Inferences
- The two headline datasets measure different populations. Carta covers equity-managed US startups, which skew toward venture-track companies. Atlas covers Delaware C-corps formed via Stripe and relies on self-reported headcount. Neither captures the many indie apps run as LLCs or sole proprietorships, so the true share of solo builders among *app businesses* is probably higher than Carta's 36%.
- Carta's numbers point to a clear split: solo founders are about a third of formations but only about 15% of priced-round dollars. Solo builders should plan for revenue funding (bootstrapping or customer cash) by default, not treat it as a fallback.
- The rise in solo share lines up in time with AI coding tools (2023–2026). Stripe's faster first-customer data (8% → 20% within 30 days) fits that story, but the cited sources do not establish causation.

### Gaps
- Could not read Carta's full report. The secondary claim that solo founders' median ownership at exit was "75% greater" than lead founders' in multi-founder companies is unverified.
- No reliable global (non-US) solo-founder share was found.
- A secondary claim that "solo founders represent 42% of companies exceeding $1M revenue" (attributed to a "SoftwareSeni 2025 benchmarking study") could not be traced to a primary source. Do not use it.

## Q2. How rare is reaching $1M ARR, solo or otherwise, and how long does it take bootstrapped?

### Takeaway
Reaching $1M ARR is a top-1%-to-top-4% outcome in every distribution found. Only 4.6% of new subscription apps reach even $10K MRR ($120K ARR) within two years (RevenueCat 2026). About 1% of TrustMRR-listed startups sit in the $1M+ band. Bootstrapped SaaS companies that do get there take a median of roughly 3–3.5 years; top-quartile bootstrappers take about 2 years. Stripe data shows the count of $1M+ solopreneurs more than doubled from 2023 to 2025. The gains are concentrated at the top while median solo revenue fell.

### Cited Findings
**Mobile/subscription apps (RevenueCat)**
- **RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026** (115,000+ apps, $16B+ revenue, covering 2025): only **4.6% of newly launched apps reach $10,000 monthly revenue within two years**. The top 10% of apps grew **306%+**, against a median year-over-year MRR growth of **5.3%**. — [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [RevenueCat 2026 summary blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026); [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top-news)
- **2026 concentration (secondary write-ups):** **57.7%** of new apps never reach $1,000 in total revenue, and the **top 10% capture 94.5%** of subscription revenue. One source gives a median app income of **$492/month (down 22% YoY)**; another gives a median of about **$72/month one year after launch**. These conflict, probably because they measure different things. — [PPC Land, "The app middle class is dying"](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/); [DEV Community, "why new apps get 3%"](https://dev.to/axrisi/app-economy-2026-why-new-apps-get-3-of-subscription-revenue-3cj8)
- **RevenueCat 2025** (75,000+ apps, $10B+ tracked revenue): roughly **20–25%** of apps reach $1,000 monthly revenue within two years of release, and about **5%** exceed $10,000 monthly revenue. The **top 5% of apps earn ~400x** what the bottom quartile earns; the 2024 edition put this at ~200x. — [GIGAZINE summary, Mar 18, 2025](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250318-mobile-apps-subscriptions-revenue/); [RevenueCat on X](https://x.com/RevenueCat/status/1901688922595320016); [TechCrunch, Mar 12, 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/12/most-subscription-mobile-apps-dont-make-money-new-report-shows/)

**Verified indie revenue databases (TrustMRR)**
- **TrustMRR live stats page** (snapshot at time of indexing, 2026; the band metric is unclear and may be total or lifetime revenue): $0–1k **67.5%**; $1k–10k **17.1%**; $10k–100k **10.3%**; $100k–1M **4.0%**; **$1M+ 1.0%**. — [TrustMRR stats](https://trustmrr.com/stats)
- **Crawlora's scrape of ~12,900 TrustMRR startups, by verified MRR:** under $1K **90.5%** (11,714); $1K–10K **7.1%** (920); $10K–50K **1.9%** (249); $50K–250K **0.4%** (53); **$250K+ MRR (≈$3M+ ARR) 0.1%** (12). Crawlora itself warns about TrustMRR's selection bias. — [Crawlora TrustMRR dataset](https://crawlora.net/datasets/trustmrr)
- **Late-2025 analysis of TrustMRR:** 81% of listed startups earned at least $1 lifetime, 44% earned at least $1,000 lifetime, and **4% earned at least $1,000,000 lifetime**. Median MRR across all tracked startups was **$334/month**. — [Quasa, "Startup Reality: The Hard Numbers from TrustMRR"](https://quasa.io/media/startup-reality-the-hard-numbers-from-trustmrr)
- **One analysis found 11,630 active listings, of which 5,596 earned anything in the last 30 days.** — [DEV Community, "I pulled verified revenue for 5,596 startups"](https://dev.to/rudratosh/i-pulled-verified-revenue-for-5596-startups-heres-exactly-what-to-build-in-2026-11-benchmarks-4eij)
- A separate Substack analysis of **986 solo startups** (cleaned TrustMRR data) found that **528 had positive revenue** and that a handful of top projects account for a disproportionate share of the total. — [One Million Goal, "What 986 Solo Startups Actually Look Like"](https://www.onemilliongoal.com/p/what-986-solo-startups-actually-look)

**Bootstrapped/independent SaaS (MicroConf)**
- **MicroConf 2024 State of Independent SaaS report** (4th annual, July 2024; respondents in 41 countries and 265 cities): three-founder teams grow **2–3x faster**; hires fell **30%** in 2023; **6% fewer** founders sought funding. — [MicroConf on X](https://x.com/MicroConf/status/1811478998217634242)
- Full-time founders grow **2.2x faster** than part-time founders, and developer-led teams grow **1.7x faster**. **28%** of independent SaaS companies are under **$1,000 MRR**, the single largest group. Targeting enterprise customers correlates with the fastest month-over-month growth (~27%). — [MicroConf $0–10K ARR page](https://microconf.com/founders/0-10k-arr); [MicroConf $1M+ ARR page](https://microconf.com/founders/1m-arr)

- **ChartMogul/Dealroom "Bootstrapped vs VC-backed" (2024; 2,500+ SaaS companies):** top-quartile bootstrapped companies reach $1M ARR in about **2 years**, only about **4 months slower** than VC-backed peers. **Stripe data on $1M+ solopreneurs and AI speed**
- **Stripe Economics, "The age of the solopreneur" (2026):** **more than twice as many solopreneurs earned over $1M in 2025 as in 2023**, and close to **three times as many** crossed **$5M and $10M**. - **Stripe 2024 annual letter (Feb 27, 2025):** the top 100 AI companies on Stripe reached **$5M annualized revenue in a median of 24 months** from founding, vs **37 months** for the top 100 SaaS companies in 2018. Note that the cohorts differ. — [TechCrunch, Feb 27, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/27/stripe-ceo-says-ai-startups-are-growing-faster-than-saas-ever-did-and-calling-them-wrappers-misses-the-point); [Stripe newsroom 2024 update](https://stripe.com/en-pl/newsroom/news/stripe-2024-update)
- **Extreme AI-era outlier (illustration only):** Base44, a solo-built AI app builder founded by Maor Shlomo, was acquired by Wix on **June 18, 2025** for about **$80M in cash** plus earn-outs through 2029. Team size at acquisition is reported as six employees (Calcalist) or as a first hire only weeks before the deal (other sources). — [Calcalist Tech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/s1iflnlelx); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/vibe-coding-base44-wix-avishai-abrahami-maor-shlomo/91267959); [No Code Exits](https://nocodeexits.substack.com/p/how-maor-shlomo-sold-base44-for-80m)

### Inferences
- If only 4.6% of new apps reach $120K ARR within two years (RevenueCat 2026), the share reaching $1M ARR is almost certainly well under that, plausibly around or below 1%. TrustMRR's 1.0% in the "$1M+" band fits, and that is a self-selected, success-skewed population. This is an inference, not a reported figure.
- A realistic bootstrapped timeline to $1M ARR, for companies that get there, is about 2 years (top quartile) to 3.5 years (median), per ChartMogul. Most indie-hacker "$1M in months" stories are top-0.1% outliers or one-time revenue spikes.
- Stripe's data shows a barbell. More $1M+ solopreneurs than ever, but a falling median. AI tools raise the ceiling for the top decile and also flood every niche with competitors.
- MicroConf's finding that three-founder teams grow 2–3x faster, and full-time founders 2.2x faster, is direct counter-evidence to the "solo is optimal" narrative. Solo is viable but slower on average. The one solo advantage that does show up in the data is ownership.

- No dataset isolates *solo* founders' time to $1M ARR. ChartMogul's split is bootstrapped vs VC, not by team size.
- Could not confirm the MicroConf median ARR or the "~17% average MoM growth" figure that a podcast host cited. Both depend on the cohort.
- Indie Hackers' own historic revenue distribution was not retrieved. ## Q3. What playbooks and principles do successful solo builders and educators describe?

### Takeaway
The recurring playbook is small, cheap, fast, and distribution-led. Start with a small product with one acquisition channel (Walling's stair-step). Run many cheap experiments and keep the winners (Levels' 12 startups in 12 months, Vassallo's small bets). Use boring, simple tech and AI coding tools to ship in hours or days. Price for revenue from day one. Watch churn, because churn sets a hard ceiling on MRR (Cohen's "Max MRR"). Verified revenue and building in public are now distribution channels in their own right.

### Cited Findings
**Rob Walling: stair-step approach and The SaaS Playbook**
- The stair-step is a three-step framework to "build up reserves as a bootstrapped founder, buy back your time and ultimately quit your day job." Step 1: **don't build a standalone SaaS first**; build a small product, typically a one-time sale with a single acquisition channel. Step 2: repeat until the combined income replaces your day job. Step 3: build a standalone SaaS you fully own and control. Originally a March 2015 blog post, re-shared by MicroConf. — [MicroConf, "The Stair Step Approach to Bootstrapping"](https://microconf.com/latest/the-stairstep-approach-to-bootstrapping); [Business Book Club summary](https://businessbookclub.substack.com/p/saas-playbook-summary-stair-step-method)
- From The SaaS Playbook, per summaries: thousands of profitable SaaS companies have never raised outside funding. **Most bootstrapped founders underprice** because they fear losing customers. **Acquisition is pointless if the product leaks**, so fix churn before scaling marketing. Walling's own path ran from consulting, to single-channel products, to the SaaS Drip. — [Founder Casebook](https://foundercasebook.com/guides/rob-walling-microconf-bootstrapped-saas); [Howtoes summary (Jun 2025)](https://howtoes.blog/2025/06/12/the-saas-playbook-complete-book-summary-all-key-ideas/)

**Jason Cohen (A Smart Bear, WP Engine): churn sets the ceiling**
- **"Max MRR: Your growth ceiling" (July 2025):** a company's revenue ceiling can be predicted from new revenue and churn. Even at a constant churn *rate*, the *dollars* lost grow with the base, so growth slows as MRR approaches Max MRR. The only ways to raise the ceiling are more new revenue or lower churn. — [A Smart Bear, Max MRR](https://longform.asmartbear.com/max-mrr/); companion essay [What to do when growth tapers off](https://longform.asmartbear.com/growth-asymptote/)
- **Customer-count form of the same math:** maximum customers = new customers per month ÷ monthly churn rate. For example, 100 new customers/month at 5% churn gives a ceiling of 2,000 customers. — [ChartMogul, "The SaaS growth ceiling"](https://blog.chartmogul.com/saas-growth-ceiling/)
- **Worked example (Smart Bear Live, July 2026):** a SaaS at $32K MRR with 9% monthly churn has a hard ceiling around $40K MRR, so it cannot reach $100K without structural change. This comes from the episode summary. — [Smart Bear Live](https://www.podengine.ai/podcasts/smart-bear-live)
- Cohen bootstrapped all his companies. Two years into WP Engine he raised angel and then institutional money. — [Indie Hackers AMA with Jason Cohen](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/im-jason-cohen-founder-of-wp-engine-ama-6d28f97cfb)

**Pieter Levels (levelsio): ship many small bets, fast, in public**
- In **2014**, after his income fell, Levels committed publicly to launching **12 startups in 12 months**, one per month. — [levels.io, "I'm Launching 12 Startups in 12 Months"](https://levels.io/12-startups-12-months); full list at [levels.io/projects](https://levels.io/projects)
- He has no co-founders, employees, or investors. His self-reported portfolio revenue is about **$3M+/year** (around **$250K/month** as of mid-2025). These are founder-published figures, not audited. His stack is described as vanilla PHP. — [Supabird profile](https://supabird.io/articles/from-spreadsheet-to-3-6m-year-the-story-of-pieter-levels); [Nomadic Blueprint (2026)](https://nomadicblueprint.com/case-studies/pieter-levels)
- **AI-era version of the same playbook (2025):** fly.pieter.com was reportedly built in about three hours by prompting Cursor. — [Yespress profile](https://yespress.io/pieter-levels); [Streakr playbook](https://streakr.co/playbook/pieter-levels)

**Daniel Vassallo: portfolio of small bets**
- Spread effort across several low-risk, cheap-to-test projects instead of one big startup bet. Limit downside, prefer low operating costs, and drop losers quickly. Small Bets has become a community and class library (54 recorded classes; instructors include Arvid Kahl and Sahil Lavingia), with Louie Bacaj joining as partner in 2023. — [Small Bets](https://smallbets.com/); [Bootstrapped Founder ep. 166](https://tbf.fm/episodes/166-a-conversation-with-daniel-vassallo); [Michael Lin review](https://www.michaellinwrites.com/p/my-review-of-daniel-vassallos-class)

**Verified revenue and building in public as distribution (Marc Lou / TrustMRR)**
- TrustMRR launched in late 2025 as "the database of verified startup revenues": founders upload a **read-only Stripe API key** and get a public verified-revenue page plus a dofollow backlink. — [Marc Lou on X (launch)](https://x.com/marclou/status/1983985733095325873)
- At one year old (~late Oct/Nov 2026), Marc Lou reported **4,000,000 visitors, 208 startups acquired** through the marketplace, and **$427,610 revenue (90% margin)** for TrustMRR. It was a "vibe-coded leaderboard" a year earlier. — [Marc Lou on X (1st birthday)](https://x.com/marclou/status/2116155913904259373)
- Verified revenue reached **$1.5B**: Stripe $1.49B, RevenueCat $8.5M, Superwall $3.8M, Whop $3.2M, LemonSqueezy $2.9M, Polar $1.6M, Paddle $1.5M. **59% of startups use Stripe, accounting for 98% of verified revenue.** — [Marc Lou on X ($1.5B)](https://x.com/marclou/status/2077736510657626203)

**Team structure and growth data (MicroConf 2024)**
- Full-time founders grow **2.2x faster** than part-time founders. **Developer-led** teams grow **1.7x faster**. **Enterprise-targeting** correlates with the fastest MoM growth (~27%). — [MicroConf $1M+ ARR page](https://microconf.com/founders/1m-arr); [MicroConf $0–10K ARR page](https://microconf.com/founders/0-10k-arr)

**AI coding tools (2024–2026)**
- Stripe's 2024 letter named AI-native products growing at unprecedented speed: Cursor at over $100M revenue, Lovable at $17M ARR "in just 3 months," Bolt at $20M ARR in two months. — [TechCrunch, Feb 27, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/27/stripe-ceo-says-ai-startups-are-growing-faster-than-saas-ever-did-and-calling-them-wrappers-misses-the-point)
- Stripe's 2025 letter: 20% of Atlas startups got a first paying customer within 30 days (vs 8% in 2020). — [Stripe 2025 annual letter](https://stripe.com/annual-updates/2025)
- Base44 (solo, ~6 months from launch to an $80M exit, 2025) is the clearest case of AI-native solo velocity. — [Calcalist Tech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/s1iflnlelx)

### Inferences
- **Synthesized playbook:**
  1. Start small, with one acquisition channel (stair-step).
  2. Run cheap, time-boxed experiments and kill losers quickly (12-in-12, small bets).
  3. Use simple tech and AI coding tools so a launch costs hours, not months.
  4. Charge from day one and price above your instinct, since underpricing is the default error.
  5. Fix churn before pouring money into acquisition, because churn defines your maximum size (Max MRR).
  6. Pick a market where customers pay more and churn less. B2B and enterprise-leaning products grow fastest in MicroConf's data.
  7. Make revenue public and verifiable. It now works as both trust and distribution (backlinks, leaderboards, acquisition marketplace).
- **Applying Cohen's math to $1M ARR:** you need about $83K MRR. At 5% monthly churn, that requires about $4.2K of new MRR every month just to *hold* $83K. At 2% churn, about $1.7K/month. Low-churn, higher-ARPU B2B niches are structurally easier for a solo founder to push to $1M than high-churn, low-price consumer apps. (Arithmetic inference from the Max MRR formula, not a sourced figure.)
- **Solo vs. team:** solo builders give up speed (MicroConf: trios grow faster) in exchange for ownership and low burn. Automation, AI agents, and contractors are the levers that close some of the speed gap.

- Arvid Kahl's *Zero to Sold* / *The Embedded Entrepreneur*: "audience-first," embedding in a community to find problems. (Search only confirmed that he wrote both books and built and sold FeedbackPanda: [thebootstrappedfounder.com](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/about-me/).)
- Justin Jackson's guidance (Transistor.fm co-founder, "build a business, not a startup").
- Paul Graham's relevant essays ("Do Things That Don't Scale," "How to Get Startup Ideas").
- Pieter Levels' *MAKE* book specifics, and the oft-cited "PHP + jQuery + SQLite" stack. Search confirmed only "vanilla PHP."
- Tony Dinh's TypingMind revenue (no reliable figure found). Marc Lou's ShipFast revenue (not searched; other researchers are covering founder profiles).
- No quantitative data was found comparing channel effectiveness for solo founders (SEO vs. programmatic SEO vs. X vs. app stores vs. marketplaces like Shopify, Chrome, WordPress, or Slack), or the share of $1M solo businesses that are B2B vs. consumer.
- No data on the effect of annual plans or price increases specific to solo SaaS.

## Q4. What are the risks, failure modes, and counter-evidence (survivorship bias, fake revenue, platform dependency, burnout)?

### Takeaway
The public picture of solo $1M businesses is heavily filtered. Most indie projects earn little or nothing, and success is extremely concentrated. Platform dependency is the most common catastrophic risk: an API price change, a search algorithm update, an app-store or marketplace policy, or a foundation-model company shipping your feature. Fake revenue screenshots were common enough to spawn Stripe-API-verified leaderboards (TrustMRR, late 2025). Those only verify payment-processor revenue, not profit or real buyers.

### Cited Findings
**Survivorship bias and the base rate**
- One builder's published four-year ledger shows **26 projects, $115K total revenue; 18 of 26 made exactly $0**. Most of the revenue came from a tiny subset. — [buildmvpfast, "Indie Hacking's Real Numbers"](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/indie-hacking-real-numbers-4-years-26-projects-115k-2026)
- About **2.4%** of indie hacker products exceed **$100K ARR**. The same analysis argues this does *not* mean 97.6% "fail," and that the "90% of startups fail" statistic is itself shaky. — [Tech Startups, Sep 28, 2026](https://techstartups.com/2026/09/28/what-percentage-of-indie-hacker-products-make-100k-in-arr/)
- Indie maker datasets warn that median revenue figures cover only makers who publish numbers, which introduces strong survivorship bias. One dataset of 2,703 profiles marks only 4% as shut down, which likely reflects who keeps profiles up rather than the true failure rate. — [Indie Maker List statistics](https://indiemakerlist.com/research/indie-hacker-statistics/)
- Indie Hackers community pushback: screenshots show new revenue but rarely churn, and the feed shows only "the shining and successful individuals." — [Indie Hackers, "I'm DONE with all these Success Stories"](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-m-done-with-all-these-success-stories-online-46084c4296)
- Revenue is concentrated even inside verified datasets. Four products account for just under 90% of the verified lifetime revenue on Marc Lou's TrustMRR profile, and a handful of projects account for a disproportionate share of TrustMRR's total. — [Promptway](https://promptway.com/blog/marc-lou-24-hour-app); [One Million Goal](https://www.onemilliongoal.com/p/what-986-solo-startups-actually-look)
- In apps, the top 10% capture about 94.5% of subscription revenue (RevenueCat 2026 via secondary), and the top 5% earn about 400x the bottom quartile (RevenueCat 2025). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/); [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250318-mobile-apps-subscriptions-revenue/)
- The median solo-founded Atlas startup's six-month revenue fell 23% in 2025 while the top decile rose 19%. — [Earning Living Online](https://www.earninglivingonline.com/stripe-solopreneur-revenue-gap-top-median-2026/)

**Fake and inflated revenue, and the verification response**
- Marc Lou launched TrustMRR ("Bye Bye fake MRR screenshots") in late 2025, reportedly after a Pieter Levels tweet about fake MRR screenshots. It verifies revenue via read-only Stripe keys, and Lou claims "there's no way to game the system." — [Marc Lou on X](https://x.com/marclou/status/1983985733095325873); [Quasa](https://quasa.io/media/how-youtuber-and-indie-hacker-marc-lou-tackled-the-fake-revenue-screenshot-problem)
- **Limits:** verification covers payment-processor revenue only. 98% of verified revenue is Stripe. It does not establish profit or costs, or that buyers are genuine (e.g., self-purchases or related-party revenue). Figures vary across snapshots ($1.2B on the site page vs. $1.5B in Lou's post). — [Marc Lou on X ($1.5B)](https://x.com/marclou/status/2077736510657626203); [IdeaIndex TrustMRR case study](https://www.ideaindex.so/case-studies/trustmrr)
- Self-reported revenue of famous solo founders (e.g., Levels' ~$3M/yr) is founder-published, not audited. — [Nomadic Blueprint](https://nomadicblueprint.com/case-studies/pieter-levels)

**Platform dependency (case studies)**
- **Apollo / Reddit API (2023):** Reddit's new API pricing of **$0.24 per 1,000 calls** would have cost Apollo about **$20M/year**: 7 billion requests a month, about $1.7M/month. Christian Selig estimated about $2.50/user/month in cost vs. Reddit's own ~$0.12. Apollo shut down **June 30, 2023**, and other third-party Reddit apps followed. Apollo reportedly had about 50,000 paying users, or roughly $500K/yr in revenue, far below the bill. — [TechCrunch, Jun 8, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/08/popular-third-party-reddit-app-apollo-is-shutting-down-as-a-result-of-reddits-new-api-pricing/); [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/135875/apollo-reddit-app-shutdown-api?amphtml=1); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apollo-and-other-popular-third-party-reddit-apps-have-shut-down-123149140.html)
- **Google Helpful Content Update (Sep 14–28, 2023):** HouseFresh, an independent air-purifier review site, lost about **95%** of its Google traffic (≈4,000 → ≈200 daily visitors), and a March 2024 update caused a further ~91% loss. Thedesk.net, housefresh.com, theworldtravelguy.com, and thrillist.com each lost over 60% of search traffic while Reddit and large brands gained. Mediavine counted **795 publishers** with negative impact. Google did not roll the update back. HouseFresh reported a partial recovery in Oct 2025. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-concludes-rollout-of-september-2023-helpful-content-update); [Tourscanner analysis](https://tourscanner.com/google-helpful-content-update-impact-on-independent-publishers); [PPC Land on HouseFresh recovery](https://ppc.land/housefresh-achieves-notable-traffic-recovery-after-google-algorithm-impacts-2/); [Mediavine](https://www.mediavine.com/?p=44954)
- **X/Twitter API:** repeated price hikes and limit cuts hurt indie tools such as KTool. Its founder called X "a risk for makers" because the API lacks stability. In 2026 X moved to a pay-per-use API model. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/x-is-about-to-double-its-api-fees-y3CoZpvTNU52BSta7h5c); [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/articles/x-api-pay-per-use-pricing-indie-hackers)
- **Meta and Shopify:** a Meta third-party API shutdown hurt PostMyParty, and a Shopify policy change restricted CartHook. Piggybacking on a platform cuts both ways. — [Micro SaaS Bytes, "Platform Dependency Risks"](https://microsaasbytes.substack.com/p/platform-risks-for-indie-hackers)
- **Foundation-model "sherlocking":** an Indie Hackers founder's Codex-related mobile product reportedly reached about $9K/month before OpenAI shipped overlapping functionality. Features increasingly appear natively in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or Cursor, so thin wrappers face short windows. — [Steal What Works](https://stealwhatworks.com/blogs/news/indie-hacking-too-crowded)

**Team-size counter-evidence**
- Three-founder teams grow 2–3x faster than other configurations, and full-time founders 2.2x faster than part-time (MicroConf 2024). — [MicroConf on X](https://x.com/MicroConf/status/1811478998217634242); [MicroConf $1M+ page](https://microconf.com/founders/1m-arr)
- Solo founders get a small share of VC dollars: ~14.7% of priced-round cash vs ~30–35% of formations (Carta 2024 cohort, via secondary). — [High Street Equity Partners](https://hsep.substack.com/p/solo-founders)
- **Loneliness is a real cost even in success stories.** Base44's Maor Shlomo said: "Yes, absolutely I felt lonely." — [Calcalist Tech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/rkogikc9le)

### Inferences
- **Risk-management heuristics that follow from these cases:**
  - Avoid a single point of distribution failure, especially Google SEO alone, one API, or one marketplace.
  - Own the customer relationship (email list, direct billing).
  - Price so that platform costs can be absorbed. Apollo's ~$2.50/user cost against its subscription price was fatal.
  - In AI, build where the model vendor is unlikely to compete: workflow depth, proprietary data, vertical niches.
- Treat any unverified revenue screenshot as marketing. Even "verified" revenue is gross processor revenue, often lifetime rather than ARR, from a self-selected population.
- Churn is the quiet killer behind many "stalled at $X MRR" stories (Cohen's Max MRR). Screenshots that show new MRR without churn overstate trajectory.
- **Bus factor:** a solo founder is a single point of failure for support, operations, and security, which also lowers acquisition multiples. See Q5, where acquirers price in owner dependence via SDE multiples and earnouts.

### Gaps
- **Burnout data:** no reliable solo-specific burnout statistic was found. A "Founder Reports" survey (May 2025; 227 entrepreneurs in 46 countries) reportedly found 26.9% loneliness, 34.4% burnout, and 87.7% reporting at least one mental-health issue. The primary URL could not be confirmed; one possible secondary source is [The Lonely Entrepreneur](https://lonelyentrepreneur.com/founder-mental-health-gap/). Figures of 52–54% burnout among solo founders appear in blogs without traceable studies, so do not use them.
- No quantitative data on support load or bus-factor incidents for solo SaaS.
- No primary data on App Store policy shocks to solo developers (e.g., review rejections, commission changes) was retrieved.
- No Hacker News thread was surfaced by search. HN commentary on survivorship bias is not cited here.

## Q5. How do solo businesses exit, and at what multiples (2024–2026)?

### Takeaway
Most solo and small SaaS exits happen on self-serve marketplaces (Acquire.com, Flippa, and now TrustMRR's marketplace) or through brokers (FE International, Empire Flippers, Quiet Light). They are priced on profit or SDE rather than revenue. On Acquire.com the median SaaS sale was about 3.9x annual profit in 2024–2025, within a typical 1x–6x range; Flippa's H1 2026 average was about 2.5x profit. Above about $1M ARR, clean profitable businesses can command about 4–6x ARR via brokers. AI-native strategic exits like Base44 are rare outliers.

### Cited Findings
- **Acquire.com (2024 and 2025, SaaS under $10M):** median confirmed SaaS sale about **3.9x annual profit**. The average is in the low-to-mid 4x range. Most deals fall between **~1x and 6x profit**, with a few standouts above 10x. Profitable SaaS listings averaged a **71% profit margin**. Average time on market was about **81 days**, with most smaller deals closing within about 90 days. — [Acquire.com Annual Report 2025 (PDF)](https://blog.acquire.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/acquire-annual-report-2025.pdf); [Acquire.com Biannual Multiples Report, Jan 2026](https://blog.acquire.com/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-jan-2026/) (figures via search summaries; PDF returned only fragments)
- **Older data point:** about **4.3x** average for bootstrapped SaaS under $10M (Acquire.com, Jan 2024 data). — [Livmo summary](https://livmo.com/blog/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-feb-2023-livmo-summary/)
- **Flippa (H1 2026):** average SaaS profit multiple **2.47x**; top quartile **4.06x**. Its sample is not directly comparable to Acquire.com's. — reported via [Acquire.com valuation multiples article](https://blog.acquire.com/business-valuation-multiples-by-industry/) / [movemrr](https://movemrr.com/blog/saas-valuation-multiples/) (search summary)
- **Broker benchmarks (secondary, attributed to FE International / Quiet Light):**
  - Under $1M ARR: about **2.5x–4x** ARR, or **2.5x–4x SDE** (≈1x–3x ARR). The basis is inconsistent across sources.
  - $1M–$5M ARR: about **4x–6x ARR** for clean, profitable businesses (other sources say 3x–6x).
  - Software Equity Group Q2 2026: private SaaS M&A clears near **4.0x revenue**; public SaaS trades around **3.2x** trailing revenue.
  - Most small SaaS deals in 2026 combine cash at signing with escrow and an earnout, which lowers the effective multiple.
  — [Livmo, SaaS Valuation Multiples 2026](https://livmo.com/blog/saas-valuation-multiples-2026/); [Axial](https://www.axial.net/forum/saas-multiples/); [Appsonbid](https://www.appsonbid.com/blog/micro-saas-valuation-multiples-2026)
- **Small deals are often priced on SDE, not ARR,** because the seller is still central to daily operations. — [Livmo, How to Value a Micro-SaaS](https://livmo.com/blog/micro-saas-valuation/)
- **TrustMRR marketplace:** it entered startup acquisitions in Dec 2025, selling verified-revenue businesses, and reported **208 startups acquired** by its one-year mark (~late 2026). — [IdeaIndex](https://www.ideaindex.so/case-studies/trustmrr); [Marc Lou on X](https://x.com/marclou/status/2116155913904259373)
- **Strategic AI outlier:** Base44 sold to Wix for about **$80M cash** plus earn-outs through 2029, roughly six months after launch (June 2025). Calcalist later reported the total could reach ~$280M with performance payments and that Base44 reported ~$200M ARR in Aug 2026. Another source says ~$150M ARR by May 2026. These later figures are unverified and conflicting. — [Calcalist Tech (deal)](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/s1iflnlelx); [Calcalist Tech (earn-out update)](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/fu5xrwt7i); [Visionary Talks](https://visionarytalks.com/who-owns-base44/)
- Arvid Kahl (author of *Zero to Sold*) built and sold FeedbackPanda, an example of the educator-founder exit path. — [The Bootstrapped Founder, About](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/about-me/)

### Inferences
- **Rule of thumb for a solo $1M-ARR SaaS:** at a 70% margin (≈$700K profit), the Acquire.com median of ~3.9x profit implies roughly **$2.7M**. The broker range of 4–6x ARR for clean $1M+ businesses implies **$4–6M**. The gap reflects buyer type (individual vs. strategic/PE), owner dependence, churn, growth, and deal structure. (Arithmetic inference.)
- Solo founders can raise their multiple by reducing owner dependence: documented SOPs, automated support, and low churn. Bus-factor risk shows up directly in SDE-based pricing and earnouts.
- Verified-revenue marketplaces (TrustMRR, Acquire.com's financial verification) are lowering transaction friction for sub-$1M solo businesses. That likely increases liquidity at the low end but does not obviously raise multiples.

### Gaps
- No primary data from **Empire Flippers, Microns, or FE International** for 2025–2026 was retrieved. Broker figures above are secondary attributions.
- Could not read Acquire.com's 2025 PDF or the Jan 2026 biannual report directly. Exact medians by revenue band (e.g., $1M+ ARR deals) are unconfirmed.
- No dataset on what share of solo $1M businesses exit vs. stay as lifestyle businesses.
