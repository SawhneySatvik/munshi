# Solo Developers Who Scaled Mobile Apps, Desktop/Mac Apps and Games to $1M+ (as of November 2026)

Research notes for the report writer. Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[Official]** = developer or publisher statement / developer's own blog
- **[Self-reported]** = founder said it in an interview, podcast, Reddit/X post (unaudited)
- **[Estimate]** = third-party model (Sensor Tower, Appfigures, Appmagic, Gamalytic, Boxleiter/Steam-review method, Alinea Analytics)
- **[Press]** = reported by press without a named primary document
- **[Single source]** / **[Secondhand]** = weak; use with caution

Note on method: revenuecat.com, indiehackers.com, appbot.co and adapty.io could not be fetched from this session (blocked by the session's network allowlist), so claims from those sites are taken from search-result snippets and are flagged.

## Q1. Which solo developers have credible evidence of $1M+ revenue from apps or games they built alone?

### Takeaway
The best-documented solo $1M+ cases are premium PC/console games (Stardew Valley, Papers, Please, Balatro, Manor Lords, Megabonk, Brotato, Lethal Company, Schedule I, Vampire Survivors, Undertale, early Minecraft), where unit-sales milestones are announced officially. Many of these are in the tens to hundreds of millions of dollars. For mobile and Mac apps the evidence is thinner and mostly self-reported or modeled: Flappy Bird ($50k/day in ads), Widgetsmith (an estimated $3M net by 2021), Slopes ($1M ARR), 7 Minute Workout (>$1M), Shot Pattern ($1M lifetime), Wave AI (about $7M ARR in 2026, self-reported), plus 2010s mobile hits such as Hill Climb Racing, Plague Inc., Geometry Dash, Tiny Wings and slither.io. Several famous names do not clear the bar, or are not really solo: 2048, Cal AI, Umax, Ellie, Adam Lyttle and MacWhisper are flagged below.

## Q2. What monetization models and distribution channels worked, and how did that change from the 2010s App Store era to the 2023–2026 TikTok/UGC + subscription era?

### Takeaway
2008–2016 solo mobile wins came from cheap paid apps ($0.99–$2.99) or free ad-supported games that hit the top of the App Store charts through organic virality, word of mouth and free "Lite" funnels. By the 2020s, solo mobile money shifted to subscriptions and freemium (often via RevenueCat) and to short-form social virality (Widgetsmith on TikTok). Solo PC games kept winning with low-priced premium Steam releases amplified by streamers and YouTubers, often with a publisher. The 2024–2026 "TikTok UGC + paywall" playbook is real, but its biggest documented wins are teams, not solo devs.

### Cited Findings

## Q3. What role did platform dependency play?

### Takeaway
Platform dependency decided several of these outcomes. Apollo was killed by Reddit's 2023 API pricing. Flappy Bird was pulled by its creator, and its trademark lapse let a crypto group revive the brand. Nova Launcher's acquisition by Branch led to layoffs and a blocked open-source release. Clones exploited open or derivative designs (2048, Vampire Survivors on mobile). Platform features also created opportunities: iOS 14 widgets made Widgetsmith, and COVID briefly boosted Plague Inc.

### Cited Findings
- **Apollo:** Reddit's new API pricing would have cost about $20M/yr ("7 billion requests last month… about 1.7 million dollars per month"). Selig shut down on June 30, 2023, and estimated about $250k in refunds to yearly subscribers. He reportedly offered to sell to Reddit, which wasn't interested — [Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/science/articles/popular-reddit-client-apollo-shut-194433986.html); [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2023/06/30/apollo-app-shutting-down-today); [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/news/apollo-to-shut-down-june-30th-leading-many-of-the-largest-subreddits-to-stage-a-blackout/); [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/reddits-new-api-pricing-kills-canadian-app-apollo/); other third-party Reddit apps also shut down — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apollo-and-other-popular-third-party-reddit-apps-have-shut-down-123149140.html)
- **Flappy Bird:** creator-initiated removal in Feb 2014 at peak ad revenue — [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/sanfrancisco/news/creator-of-wildly-popular-flappy-bird-game-withdraws-app-from-online-stores). A 2024 crypto-linked revival happened after the USPTO terminated his trademark — [heise](https://heise.de/-9869163); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/action/flappy-birds-og-creator-clarifies-he-didnt-sell-anything-to-the-blockchain-pushing-flappy-bird-foundation-thats-reviving-the-infamous-mobile-icon-i-also-dont-support-crypto)
- **Nova Launcher (Android, Kevin Barry):** after Branch acquired it, the Nova team was cut to one person in Aug 2024 — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/08/09/nova-launcher-faces-rocky-future-after-dev-team-layoffs/). Barry later left. The acquisition promise to open-source Nova if he left was not honored, and Barry was asked to stop the effort — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/nova-launcher-shutdown-3595611/); [OSNews](https://www.osnews.com/story/143297/nova-launchers-open-source-release-blocked-by-its-owners-despite-contractual-obligations/); [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/Nova-Launcher-is-no-more-as-its-founder-and-only-developer-has-left_id173850)
- **Bartender (Mac):** the silent ownership change worried users because the app holds Accessibility and Screen Recording permissions. The app also tried to contact the Amplitude analytics service — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/06/04/bartender-mac-app-new-owner/); [MJ Tsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2024/06/05/bartender-acquired-by-applause-group/)
- **Clones:** 2048's open code fed chart-topping clones, while Threes (paid) didn't benefit — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/03/24/clones-clones-everywhere-1024-2048-and-other-copies-of-popular-paid-game-threes-fill-the-app-stores). Vampire Survivors launched a free mobile version largely to pre-empt clones — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/-i-vampire-survivors-i-dev-credits-clones-for-surprise-mobile-release)
- **Platform tailwinds:** iOS 14 widgets plus TikTok drove Widgetsmith — [Appfigures](https://analytics.appfigures.com/resources/insights/20211224?f=5). COVID drove Plague Inc. to "a year's worth of revenue" in a short spike, followed by collapse — [Mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/what-happens-to-a-game-about-pandemics-when-theres-an-actual-pandemic/)

### Inferences
- Third-party clients built on another company's API (Apollo, the Reddit apps, and per general reporting Twitter clients) carry existential risk regardless of revenue. Apollo's roughly $500k/yr could not cover a roughly $20M/yr bill.
- Selling a solo app transfers platform and trust risk to the users (Bartender, Nova). In both cases the community reaction focused on broken implicit promises.

## Q4. What happened after success: stayed solo, hired, sold, burned out?

### Takeaway
Outcomes split four ways: (1) stay solo or near-solo and keep updating (Barone, Pope, LocalThunk, Topala, David Smith, Herbert, Duffett, Mohrer, Selig); (2) grow into a studio (Mojang/Notch, Fingersoft, Ndemic, poncle, TVGS, Blobfish handing development to Evil Empire); (3) sell (Wordle to NYT, Bartender to Applause, Nova to Branch, 7 Minute Workout to Wahoo, Mojang to Microsoft); (4) withdraw or burn out (Flappy Bird pulled; Apollo shut down).

### Inferences
- Solo devs with premium games tend to stay small and keep updating (the long tail matters: Stardew's sales grew from 41M to about 50M in roughly 14 months, 2024–26). Developers who hit live-service or multiplayer scale (Minecraft, Schedule I, Vampire Survivors) tend to hire quickly.
- Selling is more common for utility apps (Mac/Android) than for games in this sample, and these sales often created trust backlash.
- AI coding tools (e.g., Wave's use of Claude Code) are a 2025–26 factor letting subscription apps stay one-person at multi-million ARR. This rests on a single self-reported case.
