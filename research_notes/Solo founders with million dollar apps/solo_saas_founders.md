# Solo (one-person) founders of web apps / SaaS that reached $1M+ revenue: the classic bootstrapped indie era (started about 2010–2022)

> **Research method and limits (read first).** Notes compiled 13 Nov 2026. In this session WebFetch could not reach indiehackers.com, startupsfortherestofus.com, en.ain.ua, creatoreconomy.so or builtin.com: the hosts were blocked by the sandbox network allowlist. Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine excerpts of the cited pages, not from full-page reads. Many Indie Hackers case studies are also paywalled ("IH+ Subscribers Only"). Treat exact figures as "as reported by that page". Revenue evidence is graded as follows:
> - **A**: the founder's own dashboard, blog or company post with specific numbers, corroborated elsewhere.
> - **B**: a founder statement in an interview or podcast (self-reported, unaudited).
> - **C**: a third-party estimate (Latka/ZoomInfo, aggregators).
> - **D**: an unsourced newsletter, AI-generated profile or listicle.
>
> None of the classic-era founders below turned up with a Stripe-verified TrustMRR page in my searches.
>
> AI-wrapper products launched 2023 or later (Photo AI, fly.pieter.com, ShipFast, TypingMind, HeadshotPro, PDF.ai) and mobile apps (for example Rootd) are another researcher's scope. They are mentioned only where they affect the attribution of a "$1M" claim.

## Q1. Who are the most credible, well-documented solo founders whose web/SaaS products crossed $1M ARR or $1M annual revenue?

### Takeaway
About 6–7 founders in this era are well documented as genuinely solo at the point they crossed $1M:
- Pieter Levels (Nomad List / Remote OK)
- Mike Perham (Sidekiq)
- Ivan Kutskir (Photopea)
- AJ (Carrd), solo through the first $1M only
- Jason Zigelbaum (Zigpoll)
- Brett Williams (Designjoy), a productized service rather than SaaS
- Ajay Goel (GMass), with only one employee at the time

A second tier crossed $1M only as a multi-product portfolio total, or only after small hires. That tier includes Damon Chen (Testimonial.to), Jon Yongfook (Bannerbear), Kyle Nolan (ProjectionLab), Björn Forsberg (FORSBERG+two) and Thomas Mahony (Pckgr). The genuinely solo SaaS at $1M+ is rare. Even the famous cases have revenue evidence that is mostly self-reported.

### Cited Findings

#### Tier A: solo at or through $1M, multiple corroborating sources

**1. Pieter Levels: Nomad List and Remote OK (Netherlands-born, nomadic; self-taught developer)**
- **What the products are and when they started:**
  - Nomad List 1.0 (best cities to live and work remotely) launched 29 Jul 2014. It was the 4th project of his "12 startups in 12 months" challenge, and its launch channels were Hacker News and Product Hunt — [levels.io/projects](https://levels.io/projects).
  - Remote OK (remote job board) launched 22 Feb 2015 on HN and Product Hunt, bootstrapped off Nomad List's success — [levels.io/projects](https://levels.io/projects); [levels.io/remote-ok](https://levels.io/remote-ok).
- **Launch traffic:** Nomad List hit #1 on Product Hunt and then #1 on HN. Levels estimates about 50,000 unique visitors from HN and about 12,000 from PH, and calls it "#5 most voted Product Hunt ever" (at the time) — [levels.io/product-hunt-hacker-news-number-one](https://levels.io/product-hunt-hacker-news-number-one).
- **Revenue milestones:**
  - **Early (about 2016–17, B):** an Indie Hackers interview reported Nomad List "nearing $400k/year and growing", with about $80k of that from Remote OK — [Indie Hackers interview](https://www.indiehackers.com/interview/126df0fc5e); [IH post](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/digital-nomad-to-prolific-indie-hacker-pieter-levels-c142e7e7d8).
  - **Remote OK domain purchase (C/D, secondary):** Levels reportedly paid $100K for the remoteok.com domain once the business was at about $1.5M annual revenue — [IH/Supabird "From Google Sheets to $3.6M as a solo founder"](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/from-google-sheets-to-3-6m-as-a-solo-founder-de782f722a); [Supabird](https://supabird.io/articles/from-spreadsheet-to-3-6m-year-the-story-of-pieter-levels).
  - **Aug 2024 (B):** Lex Fridman Podcast #440 (aired 20 Aug 2024); summaries put the combined portfolio at about $3M ARR, with Nomad List about $700K ARR and Remote OK over $2M ARR — [lexfridman.com/pieter-levels](https://lexfridman.com/pieter-levels/); [Recall summary](https://www.recall.it/summary/entrepreneurship/pieter-levels-programming-viral-ai-startups-and-digital-nomad-life-or-lex-fridman-podcast).
  - **Remote OK:** "has gotten to $3.4 million in revenue" per a Cheeky Pint interview transcript — [Cheeky Pint](https://share.transistor.fm/s/16e68596). Latka's figure for Remote OK is $3.4M, up from $2M in 2023 (C) — [Latka Remote OK](https://getlatka.com/companies/remote-ok).
  - **Nomad List (C, conflicting):** Latka shows a ZoomInfo estimate of about $5.3M/yr (Nov 2024) against its own $704K estimate for 2023 and a "last reported" $336K in Apr 2020 — [Latka Nomad List](https://getlatka.com/companies/nomad-list).
  - **Combined peak (D):** combined MRR reportedly peaked at about $420K in Sep 2024, but this includes AI products such as Photo AI — [secondary profiles](https://yespress.io/pieter-levels).
- **Team:** Levels lists "Employees: 1 = just me on my laptop" — [summary of his post via profile](https://ai-vibecoding-hall-of-fame.pages.dev/people/pieter-levels/). Latka's LinkedIn-based headcount for Nomad List shows 1 person (Jul 2026), 2 (Oct 2024) and 10 (2023 and earlier). This is unverified and probably counts community members or contractors who list it as their employer — [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/nomad-list).
- **Later products (out of scope):** fly.pieter.com was reported at $1M ARR as of Mar 2025 from his own posts. It is a browser game built with AI and belongs to the other researchers — [search summary of founder posts](https://dev.to/promptway/he-built-a-flight-simulator-in-three-hours-and-hit-1m-a-year-in-17-days-then-it-went-to-zero-1b1l).
- **Pricing:** Nomad List is a paid community membership. Remote OK charges employers per job post. I found no fresh primary pricing source.
- **Outcome (late 2026):** still operating as a one-person company. No sale or shutdown was found.

**2. AJ: Carrd (one-page site builder; AJ previously made HTML5 UP free templates)**
- **Origins:** launched on Product Hunt on 1 Mar 2016 as a side project. It grew out of AJ's popular free one-page templates on HTML5 UP, and AJ already had a Twitter following from his template work — [first-mrr study / search summary](https://first-mrr.com/study/carrd); [Starter Story breakdown](https://starterstory.herokuapp.com/stories/carrd-breakdown-287e625a-9fa9-452a-9f3c-b443c111a1f6).
- **Revenue milestones:**
  - **Sep 2021 AMA (B):** "accidentally bootstrapped Carrd to $1M ARR", with 2.5M+ sites, about 1.6M users and "a small funding round earlier this year" — [IH AMA](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/hi-im-aj-maker-of-carrd-and-after-2-5m-sites-1m-arr-and-a-funding-round-probably-time-for-an-ama-1b7fcf52fe); [Reddit r/SaaS mirror, 13 Sep 2021](https://lr.us.psf.lt/r/SaaS/comments/pnfzgw/hi_i_accidentally_bootstrapped_carrd_to_1m_arr_3).
  - **Funding:** later accounts say AJ raised $2M from Rainfall Ventures while already profitable, mainly for advisors and AWS engineering access — [Fathom blog](https://usefathom.com/blog/bootstrapped-to-venture-capital); [IH podcast #228 "Making $1M/yr then raising money as a solo indie hacker"](https://dev.to/theindiehackerspodcast/228-making-1m-yr-then-raising-money-as-a-solo-indie-hacker-with-aj-from-carrd).
  - **$2M ARR (B/D):** Indie Hackers profile "Growing Carrd to $2M ARR in a crowded market with zero marketing by limiting features". A growth teardown dates the $2M crossing to 2024 and says the second $1M took longer than the first — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/dbeoP4bDlEL3zzsivdgj); [Growthmap teardown](https://growthmap-www.pages.dev/blog/carrd-growth-teardown/).
- **Pricing:** free tier plus Pro plans. The base Pro plan is reported at $19/year, with 40–50% Black Friday sales. The $19 figure comes from a secondary source only — [Growthmap teardown](https://growthmap-www.pages.dev/blog/carrd-growth-teardown/); [Carrd About page](https://carrd.co/docs/general/about).
- **Team:** solo through the first $1M ARR, then the first developer was hired after advisors urged AJ to step back. AJ's own account puts the team at 4. Other sources say 2 (AJ plus "co-founder Doni"), which conflicts — [Growthmap teardown](https://growthmap-www.pages.dev/blog/carrd-growth-teardown/); [search summary of IH/Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/carrd.co).
- **Outcome:** running. It is a small team now, no longer solo.

**3. Mike Perham: Sidekiq, a Ruby background-job framework, open-core (Contributed Systems; US)**
- **Origins and pricing:** open-source Sidekiq was released in Feb 2012 ("Happy 4th Birthday, Sidekiq", 5 Feb 2016) — [mikeperham.com](https://mikeperham.com/2016/02/05/happy-birthday-sidekiq).
  - Pro was the first paid tier. Enterprise 1.0 shipped alongside Sidekiq 4.0 in 2015, by which point Pro had "many hundreds of customers" and Enterprise was nearing its first hundred. At that launch Pro became $950/yr and Enterprise was priced on a sliding scale by thread count — [mikeperham.com/2015/08/06/sidekiq-enterprise](https://mikeperham.com/2015/08/06/sidekiq-enterprise); [2015 wrap-up](https://www.mikeperham.com/2016/01/05/contributed-systems-2015/).
  - Current list prices are Pro $99/mo or $995/yr, and Enterprise from $269/mo per 100 threads. Appliance licenses are $14,995/yr (Pro) and $39,995/yr (Enterprise) — [sidekiq.org](https://sidekiq.org/); [Commercial FAQ](https://github.com/sidekiq/sidekiq/wiki/Commercial-FAQ).
- **Revenue milestones:**
  - **About $1M/yr (B):** "grew revenue to $80,000/mo without making a single hire", from an Indie Hackers text interview (about Nov 2016) and IH podcast #016 (2017) — [IH podcast 016](https://indiehackers.com/podcast/016-mike-perham-of-sidekiq); [IH interview "How charging money for pro features allowed me to quit my job"](https://www.indiehackers.com/interview/how-charging-money-for-pro-features-allowed-me-quit-my-job-6e71309457).
  - **About 2023 (B):** on Startups for the Rest of Us ep. 661, Perham said he was "closer to 10 million than 1 million" in annual revenue, with about 2,000 customers and no employees — [SFTRU ep 661](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-661-millions-in-revenue-as-a-one-person-software-company). The SaaS Unbound podcast is titled "Bootstrapping a SaaS to $7M solo" — [Metacast](https://metacast.app/podcast/saas-unbound/lPuGYxma/mike-perham-sidekiq-bootstrapping-a-saas-to-7m-solo/NUlb6nHp).
  - **Conflicting estimate (C):** Latka lists $2M (Dec 2023, citing X) — [Latka Sidekiq](https://getlatka.com/companies/sidekiq).
- **Distribution:** years of blogging on advanced Ruby topics had already given him trust in the Ruby community. The open-source library acts as the funnel to the paid tiers — [Starting & Sustaining](https://startingandsustaining.garrettdimon.com/mike-perham); [3l3c write-up](https://www.3l3c.ai/us/blog/solopreneur-marketing-strategies-usa/solo-founder-open-core).
- **Outcome:** still operating and still solo as of the latest sources. It is arguably the strongest "solo, $1M+ for a decade" case, though the product is a paid developer library rather than a hosted web app.

**4. Ivan Kutskir: Photopea (browser-based Photoshop alternative; Ukrainian developer)**
- **Revenue milestones:**
  - **2020 (B, reported by press):** about $500K. The same Apr 2021 article is headlined "makes $1M per year", so the headline and body differ — [AIN.ua, 15 Apr 2021](https://en.ain.ua/2021/04/15/photopea-history).
  - **2024 (B):** about $3M, founder-reported (disclosure dated May 2025) — [IH "Making $3M+ per year with a free product"](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/making-3m-per-year-with-a-free-product-axW4u1vB6C8f91Z3Lxu5).
  - **Latka estimates (C):** $1.5M (2023) and $2.8M (2024) — [Latka Photopea](https://getlatka.com/companies/photopea).
  - **Outlier (D):** one 2024 post claims "slightly over $200,000 a month from ads". It is treat-with-caution — [search summary](https://5to9.beehiiv.com/p/photopea-free-photoshop-alternative-making-millions).
- **Model:** about 90% of revenue comes from ads and about 10% from Premium (ad-free; about $5/mo per secondary sources) plus API/licensing deals. Revenue "has always directly corresponded to the size of the user base" — [IH/aggregated quotes](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-reddit-ama-earned-photopea-12m-monthly-users-7c66c1d9ad); [Failory interview](https://failory.com/interview/photopea).
- **Users:**
  - About 10M opens/month (2021) — [AIN.ua](https://en.ain.ua/2021/04/15/photopea-history).
  - About 1M people/day and 12M monthly users (2024) — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-reddit-ama-earned-photopea-12m-monthly-users-7c66c1d9ad).
  - Hosting reportedly costs tens of dollars per year — [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@uzmanali/this-guy-makes-1m-per-year-with-0-employees-e8d15021b625).
- **Distribution:** a late-2018 Reddit AMA (about 50K upvotes, 2.2K comments) followed by HN made traffic "skyrocket" — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-reddit-ama-earned-photopea-12m-monthly-users-7c66c1d9ad); [Gigazine 2018](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20181111-photopea-developer-ask-question).
- **Team:** zero employees.
- **Outcome:** running.

  - May 2020: a solo founder at $100K/month from 4 apps, 3 of which were sold as one-time payments — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/100k-mo-from-4-shopify-apps-solo-not-me-9ab6034f5c).

- **Name:** "+two" refers to his wife and daughter.

- **Channel:** the Shopify App Store.

**12. Thomas Mahony: Pckgr (browser-based app packaging and deployment for Microsoft Intune)**
- **Revenue milestones:**
  - About $60K/month (Starter Story) — [Starter Story via Rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/i-make-60k-month-from-the-most-boring-saas-on-the-internet).

- **Verdict:** scoped for solo management, but there was a small team at $1M. This is an edge case.

**13. Tim Bennetto: Pallyy (social media scheduling); a near-miss**
- **Revenue milestones:** stuck at about $1–2K MRR for 1.5 years, then reached $74K MRR solo (about $888K ARR) — [IH "Here's how I've built Pallyy to $74K MRR solo"](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/heres-how-i-ve-built-pallyy-to-74k-mrr-solo-a5d9c78766); [IH $2K MRR post](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/social-media-scheduling-platform-hits-2-000-mrr-after-1-5-years-653161b11a).

- **Team:** he began hiring a developer after about 4 years.
- **Verdict:** not shown at $1M.

### Inferences
- The "solo SaaS at $1M+" population from this era is small. The cases with the best documentation are Levels, Perham, Kutskir, AJ, Zigelbaum and Goel. Several of the most-cited "$1M solo" names actually reached $1M only via a multi-product portfolio (Chen, Yongfook, Høiberg, Levels' later AI products).
- The solo $1M+ businesses cluster into four archetypes:
  - **Ad- or traffic-monetized free tools:** Photopea.
  - **Open-core developer libraries:** Sidekiq.
  - **Marketplace-distributed apps:** Zigpoll and FORSBERG+two on Shopify; GMass on the Chrome/Gmail ecosystem.
  - **Communities and job boards fed by build-in-public audiences:** Nomad List and Remote OK.

  Classic horizontal SaaS (Testimonial, Bannerbear, Pallyy) tends to plateau at about $0.6–0.9M when it stays truly solo.
- International coverage is thin:
  - **Europe:** Levels (Netherlands), Kutskir (Ukraine) and probably Forsberg. Forsberg's nationality was not confirmed.
  - **Southeast Asia:** Yongfook (Singapore), only borderline.
  - **India and Latin America:** I found no verified solo $1M+ web/SaaS founder.

### Gaps

- Exact dates for Carrd's first hire and its current headcount are unresolved (2 vs 4).
- Testimonial.to's standalone revenue after 2023 is unknown.
- Bannerbear's current open-page MRR is unknown, as is which "three products" make up the $1M claim.
- Zigpoll's start year (2019) comes from Starter Story only. The dates of the $68K and $125K milestones are not pinned down.
- WordPress plugins: Elliot Condon (Advanced Custom Fields) was a famous solo WP plugin developer. ACF had 1M+ active installs and a Pro-version business, and Condon hired a few developers for support before selling to Delicious Brains. No revenue or sale price was ever disclosed, so a $1M claim cannot be verified — [WP Tavern](https://wptavern.com/delicious-brains-acquires-advanced-custom-fields-plugin); [GoDaddy Q&A](https://www.godaddy.com/garage/qa-with-elliot-condon-of-advanced-custom-fields/); [Post Status](https://poststatus.com/takeaways-from-the-acf-acquisition/). The closest were Fernando Pessagno (Argentina, solo, no revenue disclosed), an Argentine designer at about $15K MRR, and Solides (Brazil, $1M ARR by 2019 but not solo, later VC-backed) — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/nabmB9HOZvkx4J9902j6); [first-mrr Solides](https://first-mrr.com/study/solides).
- Candidates named in the brief where I found no evidence of a solo $1M+ web/SaaS product: Rob Hope, Steph Smith, Jake Lazaroff, Matt Mazur, Dru Riley, Florin Pop, Hiten Shah, Ben Orenstein, Lior Grossman, "Kristen" and Ahmed Sulaiman. Searches did not surface relevant revenue data, and none should be included without fresh evidence.

## Q2. What is the evidence for each revenue claim, and how reliable is it? (self-reported vs verified)

### Takeaway
Essentially all solo-founder $1M+ claims in this era are self-reported: interviews, podcasts, tweets and the founder's own blog. The only "hard" artifacts are founders' own open pages and company blog posts (Bannerbear, ProjectionLab, historically Levels). Latka/ZoomInfo figures frequently contradict founders by 2–7×, so they should not be used as confirmation. None of these classic-era founders appeared with a Stripe-verified TrustMRR listing in my searches.

### Cited Findings

- **How TrustMRR works:** it pulls revenue directly from Stripe, LemonSqueezy or Paddle and refreshes hourly. It ranks startups by MRR but has no solo-founder category — [exitbid TrustMRR review](https://exitbid.io/blog/trustmrr-review-2026); [lzwjava summary](https://lzwjava.github.io/trustmrr-verified-revenue-en). It is mainly used by the 2023+ AI-era cohort, not the founders above.
- **BuiltWith case:** co-founder Andrew Rogers wrote that he "needed to correct the valuations and revenue numbers being noted in Hacker News", because Gary had given cumulative customer numbers rather than concurrent subscribers — [Andrew Rogers, Medium](https://medium.com/@andrewjrogers/the-story-of-builtwith-e3bbc17c239f).

### Inferences
- The most trustworthy pattern is consistency across independent founder statements over time. Perham's $80K/mo (2017) leading to multi-millions (2023), Levels' repeated public numbers, and Bannerbear's dated milestone posts all show it. A single headline figure from a newsletter carries much less weight.
- Newsletter and beehiiv "This guy makes $X with 0 employees" pieces routinely round up, mix ARR, revenue and run-rate, and combine products. The report writer should cite founder-origin numbers and label Latka as an estimate.

### Gaps
- No audited financials, tax filings or acquisition disclosures exist for any of the Tier A founders.
- No Stripe-verified (TrustMRR-style) record was found for any classic-era solo founder.

## Q3. How did each acquire users in the first year, and what drove growth to $1M?

### Takeaway
First-year traction almost always came from an existing audience or a big launch moment (HN/PH #1, a Reddit AMA, a Product Hunt lifetime deal) or from being inside a marketplace (Shopify App Store, Gmail/Chrome). Growth to $1M then came from a compounding, mostly free channel:
- **SEO, utility traffic or viral footers:** Photopea, Carrd, Nomad List.
- **An open-source funnel:** Sidekiq.
- **App-store ranking:** Zigpoll, FORSBERG+two.

Paid ads were rarely decisive, and GMass grew faster after cutting them.

### Cited Findings
- **Levels:** #1 on Product Hunt and #1 on HN at the 2014 launch (about 50K HN and 12K PH visitors). Remote OK launched the same way in 2015 and fed off the Nomad List audience. Ongoing growth came from building in public on Twitter and community — [levels.io](https://levels.io/product-hunt-hacker-news-number-one); [levels.io/remote-ok](https://levels.io/remote-ok); [Growthmap Nomad List teardown](https://growthmap-www.pages.dev/blog/nomad-list-growth-teardown/).
- **Carrd:**
  - **First year:** an existing HTML5 UP and Twitter audience, then a 1 Mar 2016 Product Hunt launch described as the "real breakthrough".
  - **Growth driver:** the "Made with Carrd" footer on every free site acts as the primary distribution loop. The product claims "zero marketing" — [first-mrr](https://first-mrr.com/study/carrd); [Growthmap teardown](https://growthmap-www.pages.dev/blog/carrd-growth-teardown/).
- **Sidekiq:** years of Ruby blogging built trust. The free open-source library spread through the Rails ecosystem and paid Pro/Enterprise tiers converted companies — [Starting & Sustaining](https://startingandsustaining.garrettdimon.com/mike-perham).
- **Photopea:** a late-2018 Reddit AMA (about 50K upvotes) plus HN produced traffic spikes. Growth since then is organic and search-led, and revenue tracks users because 90% comes from ads — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-reddit-ama-earned-photopea-12m-monthly-users-7c66c1d9ad).
- **Zigpoll:** Shopify App Store distribution, plus doubling down on the right merchant segment — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/c4o2Tfs6mjdpip5yZhaO). A "GTM Social" substack says Zigpoll doubled MRR in 10 months with social content — [GTM Social](https://gtmsocial.substack.com/p/6-figure-saas-zigpoll-doubled-mrr?r=3btnwz).
- **GMass:** free for about the first year inside Gmail, reaching about 1M signups. Facebook ads were later cut, and revenue doubled anyway — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/interview/how-i-hit-115k-mo-with-a-status-quo-improvement-c45d11ad17); [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/gmass-inc-b9e9cf9c-98b3-418b-9b1b-b4456ce3070f).
- **Testimonial.to:** a $199 lifetime deal on Product Hunt, his Twitter following, and martech Facebook groups — [Latka interview](https://getlatka.com/companies/testimonial-io/interviews/testimonial-io-damon-chen-2021); [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/testimonial/2-000-revenue-in-7-days--MPL-_yFhZ1AvFa8Ik1X).
- **Bannerbear:** slow organic start ($1K MRR about a year in), then a deliberate year of content and SEO marketing took it from $10K to $27K MRR — [Bannerbear blog](https://bannerbear.com/blog/one-year-of-marketing-a-saas-from-10k-to-20k-mrr).
- **ProjectionLab:** building in public on the company blog and a personal-finance (FIRE) audience. Two-plus years of nights and weekends before quitting at about $200K ARR — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/kyle-nolan-finally-turned-his-side-hustle-into-his-main-hustle-at-200k-arr-but-kept-his-job-84whHWnbr6KiqdNbM8uf).
- **Pckgr:** a Reddit launch, then budget spent on Microsoft MVPs to demo the product — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/carving-out-a-1m-yr-niche-out-of-the-microsoft-ecosystem-ekxDn4Rdn8zD3uZU2asY).
- **Pallyy:** affiliates make up about 20% of revenue — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/heres-how-i-ve-built-pallyy-to-74k-mrr-solo-a5d9c78766).
- **Designjoy:** Twitter/X audience and word of mouth; ran as a side project for 3.5 years — [IH podcast #249](https://chartable.com/podcasts/the-indie-hackers-podcast/episodes/110556717-249-the-keys-to-making-1myear-as-a-solo-founder-with-brett-williams-of-designjoy).

### Inferences
- Pre-existing distribution is the single strongest predictor here. AJ had his templates audience, Perham his Ruby reputation, Levels his blog and Twitter, and Goel a prior email company (JangoMail).
- Marketplace-native products (Shopify, Gmail) reached $1M with near-zero brand-building. The trade-off is platform dependency, as shown by Microsoft launching a competitor to Pckgr.
- Time-to-$1M was typically about 4–8 years:
  - Carrd: 2016 to 2021.
  - ProjectionLab: 2021 to 2025.
  - FORSBERG+two: about 8 years.
  - Zigpoll: 2019 to about 2024–25.

  The 12–24-month "$1M solo" stories mostly belong to the 2023+ AI cohort.

### Gaps
- First-year channel attribution for Nomad List and Remote OK after launch, and for Zigpoll's first customers, is not documented with numbers in the sources I could access.
- I did not find Product Hunt vote counts for Carrd that agree with each other (1,019 vs "over 4,000").

## Q4. Did they stay solo after $1M (hire, contractors, sell)?

### Takeaway
Only a handful stayed strictly solo well past $1M: Levels, Perham, Kutskir, Zigelbaum and Williams. The rest hired a small team soon after crossing $1M or near it: Carrd (2–4 people), GMass (about 11), ProjectionLab (2+ engineers) and Pckgr. Classic-era exits from this list were rare. Baremetrics (not solo at $1M) sold for $4M, and ACF sold for an undisclosed sum.

### Cited Findings
- **Strictly solo:**
  - Levels: "Employees: 1 = just me" — [profile summarizing his post](https://ai-vibecoding-hall-of-fame.pages.dev/people/pieter-levels/).

  - Williams: solo, an "agency of one" — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/broke-the-1-5m-arr-mark-as-an-agency-of-one-e26ae36fe7).
- **Hired after or around $1M:**
  - Carrd: first developer hired after the 2021 funding; team of 2–4 — [Growthmap](https://growthmap-www.pages.dev/blog/carrd-growth-teardown/).
  - GMass: 1 founder plus 1 employee (2020), rising to 11 employees (Dec 2024) — [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/gmass).
  - ProjectionLab: growing the team and hiring a second engineer (Jan 2026) — [ProjectionLab](https://cdn.projectionlab.com/blog/were-growing-the-team).
  - Pckgr: "small team" — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/carving-out-a-1m-yr-niche-out-of-the-microsoft-ecosystem-ekxDn4Rdn8zD3uZU2asY).
  - Pallyy: began hiring a developer at $74K MRR — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/heres-how-i-ve-built-pallyy-to-74k-mrr-solo-a5d9c78766).
- **Solo plus contractors:**
  - FORSBERG+two: Indian developers built the first app — [Bootstrappers](https://bootstrappers.com/wanting-more-time-with-family-this-founder-built-a-suite-of-shopify-apps-that-generate-over-1-million-arr/).
  - ACF: Condon used freelance developers for support — [GoDaddy Q&A](https://www.godaddy.com/garage/qa-with-elliot-condon-of-advanced-custom-fields/).
  - Testimonial.to: equity-paid marketing advisor (2021) — [Latka interview](https://getlatka.com/companies/testimonial-io/interviews/testimonial-io-damon-chen-2021).

### Inferences
- Staying solo past $1M correlates with products that need little customer support per dollar: ad-supported tools, developer libraries with self-serve licensing, job boards and community memberships. Support-heavy B2B SaaS (email, scheduling, finance planning) tends to hire.

### Gaps
- Contractor and VA usage at Nomad List, Remote OK and Photopea is not documented. Latka's Nomad List headcount (10 in 2023) hints at moderators or contractors, but it is unverified.
- I found no exit data for any Tier A founder. None appear to have sold as of late 2026.

## Q5. Edge cases: founders often described as solo who actually had a co-founder or team (avoid mis-attribution)

### Takeaway
Several famous "solo $1M" stories need caveats:
- **Not solo at $1M:** Wingify/VWO (co-founded), Baremetrics (team of about 6–8 before $1M) and Pckgr (small team).
- **A co-founder exists:** FeedHive (co-founder Simon Grønborg) and BuiltWith (co-founder Andrew Rogers in the early 2010s).
- **Conflicting accounts:** Carrd ("co-founder Doni" claim).
- **Out of scope:** Rootd (mobile, non-coder) and Marc Lou, Tony Dinh and Levels' newer products (AI era).

### Cited Findings
- **Wingify / VWO (India):** often cited as Paras Chopra's bootstrapped solo success, but it was co-founded with Sparsh Gupta (some sources also list Anil Chopra) in 2009–2010. ### Inferences
- When a story says "solo founder, $1M", check three things:
  1. Is it ARR, run-rate or cumulative revenue?
  2. Is it one product or a portfolio?
  3. Was the founder solo at the moment of crossing, or only at the start?

  Most mis-attributions in this space fail one of the three.
