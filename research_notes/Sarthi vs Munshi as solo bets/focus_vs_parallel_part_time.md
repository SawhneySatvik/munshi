## Q1. Focus vs. parallel: what credible practitioners say, when two products work and when they fail, and what the data says

### Takeaway
Most practitioners say "focus" for a founder who has no traction yet, and those who run portfolios mostly agree. Even the portfolio builders (Levels, Dinh, Marc Lou, Vassallo) work in a pattern that differs from "build two new products in parallel." They ship many cheap experiments in sequence. They sell to one shared audience, often their own large personal following. They keep side products low-maintenance (one-time purchases, info products). Then they concentrate on whichever one wins. Walling, Cohen, Paul Graham and Justin Jackson argue explicitly for one top priority. The MicroConf data (full-time founders grow 2.2x faster than part-time ones) suggests that founder hours are a binding constraint. Splitting 10–15 hours a week across two products makes that constraint twice as tight.

### Inferences
- **The portfolio exemplars don't match this founder's situation.** Levels, Marc Lou and Vassallo each had (a) full-time hours, (b) a large personal audience that every new product reached for free, and (c) cheap, low-support products. Dinh's portfolio is two one-time-purchase utilities plus one SaaS that took his focus. A part-time founder with no audience, weighing a consumer subscription app (Sarthi) and a high-touch B2B SaaS for Indian shops (Munshi), has none of (a)–(c). The two ideas also share almost no distribution: global or Indian consumers via short-video and app stores, versus shopkeepers reached in person and on WhatsApp. That is the configuration in which every pro-portfolio source says portfolios fail. Only infrastructure would carry over (LLM plumbing, memory layer, billing).
- **Time-budget arithmetic.** 10–15 h/week × 13 weeks ≈ **130–195 hours in 3 months**, and ≈ **260–390 hours in 6 months**. Split across two products, each gets ~65–100 hours per quarter, before context-switching losses. That is roughly 2–2.5 full-time work-weeks per product per quarter. If MicroConf's 2.2x figure even partly reflects hours, two part-time products are each a quarter-time effort.
- **What the sources do support is "parallel validation, serial building."** Run cheap, time-boxed demand tests on both ideas for a few weeks (Levels' "ship more" applied to tests, not products). Then commit nearly all build hours to the winner (Walling, Cohen, Graham). Park the other in a documented, low-cost state. This keeps Vassallo's option value without Walling's distraction cost.
- Running two products works mainly when: one is in maintenance mode with low support (Dinh's utilities); both share an audience or channel; both share a codebase; or the founder already has distribution. None of these holds yet for Sarthi + Munshi, apart from possibly a shared AI backend.

## Q2. Side-project success stories: thresholds, hours and timelines

### Takeaway
Credible side-project-to-revenue stories exist, and they are usually **slow**. Slopes took ~9 years to reach $1M ARR. Plausible took 324 days to reach $400 MRR. Shot Pattern needed years of teacher's-hours work before $185K in 2024. The founders who quit did so at meaningful MRR: ProjectionLab went full-time at ~$23.3K MRR after first dropping to part-time at his day job. Base44 (sold for $80M after 6 months) is a strong outlier from a repeat founder. Academic evidence (Raffiee & Feng, 2014) supports keeping the day job: hybrid entrepreneurs are ~33% less likely to fail. Exact hours per week are rarely disclosed.

**Evidence on keeping the day job**
- Raffiee & Feng, "Should I Quit My Day Job?: A Hybrid Path to Entrepreneurship," *Academy of Management Journal* (Aug/Sep 2014). They studied 5,000+ entrepreneurs over ~15 years (1994–2008). Hybrid entrepreneurs, who keep their jobs while starting up, were **~33% less likely to fail** than those who quit first. The authors attribute this to staged, lower-sunk-cost testing rather than personality: hybrids were no more risk-tolerant. ## Q3. Validation methods that fit ~10–15 hours/week, and kill/continue metrics

### Takeaway
Practitioners converge on "**validate willingness to pay before building much**": Walling, Kahl, Jackson, and Walling's 2026 episode title "Building Is Not the Hard Part." The right method differs by idea type. For a consumer app (Sarthi), content-first tests (short-video hooks, fake demos, waitlists) are cheap and asynchronous, which suits a day job, but views are not payment. For B2B to Indian shops (Munshi), Indian precedents (OkCredit, Instamojo) show in-person shop visits, WhatsApp-group onboarding videos and a product simple enough to use without a demo. Willingness to pay appears mainly when the software touches money flow. Kill/continue metrics with published benchmarks include the Sean Ellis 40% "very disappointed" test, Lenny's 6-month retention benchmarks, and RevenueCat's trial-conversion and revenue base rates.

### Cited Findings

**General principles (bootstrapper educators)**

Find a community and its problems before choosing the idea, confirm willingness to pay, talk directly to people who raised the problem, and invalidate the riskiest assumptions first. His FeedbackPanda (with a partner) reached ~$55K MRR in 2 years and sold for seven figures without employees. — [TechBooks summary](https://techbooks.substack.com/p/zero-to-sold); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-to-build-an-audience-driven-business-arvid-kahl-the-embedded-entrepreneur-32c13207fd); [YesPress](https://yespress.io/arvid-kahl); [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/pdf/zero-to-sold-pdf-arvid-kahl)

- Walling, Ep. 845 (2026, Rob solo): "Lifetime Deals Revisited, **Building Is Not the Hard Part**, and **Confirming an Idea Is Worth Paying For**." ### Inferences
- **Methods that fit a day job.** Consumer content tests (script and film short videos at night, then read comments and waitlist conversions) are asynchronous and fit 10–15 h/week well. B2B shop validation needs *in-person, daytime-or-evening* visits: OkCredit's founders walked into shops. Many Indian shops stay open evenings and weekends (an inference, not sourced), which may make Munshi's field work feasible part-time. But each visit is costly in hours, so a 10–20-shop sample is realistic per quarter, not hundreds.
- **Concrete kill/continue gates for a 6–10 week test window** (synthesized; thresholds borrowed from the benchmarks above, not prescribed by any one source):
  - *Sarthi:* a waitlist or pre-order conversion from content. After a concierge or "Wizard-of-Oz" coaching MVP with 20–50 users, look for: Sean Ellis ≥40% "very disappointed" (≥25% = keep iterating); week-4 retention trending toward the consumer-SaaS "good" 6-month line (~40%); at least some users paying. The RevenueCat base rate (~5% of H&F apps reach $10K in 2 years) means that "some people liked it" is not enough.
  - *Munshi:* in 10–20 shop conversations, at least 3–5 shops agree to a paid pilot or pay something up front. Better still, the product sits on a money flow (payments, credit collection, GST/invoicing): Instamojo's lesson. Sustained weekly use without hand-holding (OkCredit's "no demo needed" bar). SMB retention benchmark ~60% at 6 months.
- **AI makes building cheap, so validation is the scarce step.** Walling's 2026 episode title ("Building Is Not the Hard Part") and Base44's 6-month arc both suggest that in 2025–26 the scarce resource for a part-timer is **distribution and evidence of payment**, not code.

## Q4. Decision frameworks for choosing between two ideas

### Takeaway
No source offers a validated "pick between two ideas" formula. Walling explicitly declines to choose for founders. The recurring criteria across YC (Jared Friedman), Paul Graham, Justin Jackson, Jason Cohen and the bootstrapper community are:
- founder–market fit and organic insight;
- **distribution you can actually reach**;
- evidence that people already spend money on the problem;
- time-to-first-dollar;
- maintenance and support load;
- ARPU vs. volume;
- platform and competitive risk.

For a part-timer, distribution reach and support load matter more than usual, because they consume scarce hours.

### Cited Findings
- **YC, Jared Friedman, "How to Get and Evaluate Startup Ideas" (Startup School, c. 2022):** ten questions. They include founder–market fit (called the most important), market size, problem acuteness, competition (most good ideas have competitors), whether you want it yourself, a recent change that enables it, existence of proxies, long-term potential, and scalability. He warns against "solutions in search of a problem" and "tar-pit ideas." The question lists differ slightly between summaries. — [Gist notes from the video](https://gist.github.com/crkrenn/3ab4024df9da10c0b9dc7379c36358e2); [MIT keynote reflection](https://www.mit.edu/~edchen93/posts/how-to-get-and-evaluate-startup-ideas/); [Recapio digest](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-get-and-evaluate-startup-ideas-startup-school-by-y-combinator)
- **Paul Graham, "How to Get Startup Ideas" (Nov 2012):** "The way to get startup ideas is not to try to think of startup ideas. It's to look for problems, preferably problems you have yourself." The most successful startups almost all begin with "organic" ideas from the founders' own experience. Many began as side projects that "just seemed cool" (per summaries). Forced brainstorming yields "sitcom ideas." — [paulgraham.com/startupideas](https://paulgraham.com/startupideas.html); [PG, "Organic Startup Ideas"](https://paulgraham.com/organic.html); [LessWrong lit review](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/55jLckKpmjSfXKjPp/how-to-get-startup-ideas-a-brief-lit-review-and-analysis)
- **Justin Jackson, distribution reachability:** "If people are willing to pay, but there's no efficient way to reach them, that's a bad market!" In a strong market that fits you, "finding good distribution channels shouldn't be a mystery." Focus on categories where people already spend money. "Every market has a limit." — [justinjackson.ca tweets](https://justinjackson.ca/tweets/page/154/); [justinjackson.ca tweets](https://justinjackson.ca/tweets/page/177/); [IH podcast #118, "The magic of choosing a great market"](https://www.indiehackers.com/podcast/118-justin-jackson-of-transistor)

- **Rob Walling, Ep. 785 (Jul 29, 2025):** he won't pick between a founder's 2–3 ideas for them. The episode also covers whether early niche positioning hurts later growth (9:38) and when to lower prices (14:51). — [SFTRFU Ep. 785](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-785-choosing-between-ai-products-building-multiple-apps-and-more-listener-questions)

- **Market-type data:** MicroConf 2024 found enterprise-targeting independent SaaS had the fastest MoM growth (~27%) (from the earlier notes). Lenny's benchmarks show SMB SaaS retains far better than consumer products (60% vs. 25–40% "good" at 6 months). — [MicroConf $1M+ page](https://microconf.com/founders/1m-arr); [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-retention-issue-29)

Merchants pay when the software touches money flow. — [Upgrowth](https://upgrowth.in/saas-pricing-packaging-strategy-india-gtm/); [ValueForStartups](https://valueforstartups.in/dukaan); [Sacra/Instamojo](https://sacra.com/chat/h/5d2cfb77-4c2d-49e8-b968-5428e29d4614/)

### Inferences
- **A scoring rubric synthesized from the sources** (an inference, not a published framework). Score each idea 1–5 on:
  1. Founder–market fit or organic insight (YC, PG).
  2. **Reachable distribution with 10–15 h/week** (Jackson). Can I get 20 qualified conversations or 1,000 targeted views this month without quitting?
  3. Evidence of existing spend (Jackson) or a money-flow hook (Instamojo).
  4. Time-to-first-dollar.
  5. Support and maintenance load per customer (Dinh).
  6. ARPU × reachable volume needed to replace salary.
  7. Platform and competitive risk (app stores, LLM vendors, well-funded incumbents).
  8. Retention ceiling of the category (Lenny).
  9. Energy and "fun," because the part-time founder must sustain it for years (Slopes took 9 years).

  Weight items 2 and 5 higher for a part-timer.
- **Applied directionally:**
  - *Sarthi (consumer AI coach):*
    - Strengths: asynchronous distribution, global USD pricing, low per-user support.
    - Weaknesses: brutal consumer base rates, low retention ceilings, heavy competition and platform risk from general-purpose assistants, and (unsourced here) sensitivity around mental-health-adjacent advice.
  - *Munshi (Indian shops):*
    - Strengths: B2B retention benchmarks are higher, there is a money-flow hook if it handles billing, credit or GST, and access to shops is a real moat if the founder has it.
    - Weaknesses: low INR ARPU, high-touch onboarding and support, crowded funded competitors, and daytime field work that may clash with the job.
- The founder's **specific unfair advantage** should probably break the tie: an existing audience, personal pain, shop-owner relationships, or language and vernacular skills. This follows YC's founder–market-fit emphasis.

- I found no Lenny Rachitsky article that specifically frames "choosing between two ideas." Only his retention benchmarks were retrieved.
- I found no quantitative study comparing B2B and B2C outcomes for *part-time solo* founders.
- The ten Friedman questions are reconstructed from secondary summaries. The exact wording was not retrieved.

## Q5. Legal and practical constraints for employed founders (India focus)

### Takeaway
India has no general statute on moonlighting. For most private-sector employees the **employment contract governs**. Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 voids *post-employment* non-competes, but Indian courts generally **uphold restrictions that apply during employment**, including exclusivity clauses (Niranjan Shankar Golikari, SC). The practical risk of a side venture is therefore **termination or a disciplinary dispute**, not an injunction after leaving. Since 2022, major Indian IT employers (Wipro, Infosys) have actively enforced dual-employment bans. On IP, the Copyright Act's Section 17(c) gives the employer copyright only in works made "in the course of employment," unless the contract says otherwise. The contract's IP-assignment wording is decisive, so founders should read it and get written carve-outs.

### Cited Findings
- **No dedicated moonlighting law.** Sector rules exist: the Factories Act, 1948, Section 60, bars a factory worker from working in two factories. Otherwise "employees are primarily governed by the terms of the contract of employment." — [ClearTax](https://news.cleartax.in/?p=8455); [The Week (Oct 16, 2022)](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2022/10/16/moonlighting-reasons-benefits-laws-in-india-sacking-employers-upset-ideas-moonlight.amp.html)
- **Enforcement wave of 2022:** Wipro fired ~300 employees for moonlighting (for competitors) in September 2022. Infosys emailed staff reminding them that dual employment is not allowed; its contract reportedly bars full- or part-time work in any business without company consent. Former Infosys director Mohandas Pai publicly disagreed that moonlighting is "cheating." — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/what-is-moonlighting-here-s-why-wipro-fired-300-employees-for-doing-it-news-225205); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/moonlighting-what-is-it-and-why-are-companies-in-india-warning-employees-against-it-1.1663311580778); [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2022/10/16/moonlighting-reasons-benefits-laws-in-india-sacking-employers-upset-ideas-moonlight.amp.html)
- **Section 27 and the timing distinction:**
  - Section 27 makes agreements in restraint of trade void, so post-employment non-competes are generally unenforceable. — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/enforceability-non-compete-clauses-india/); [BananaIP](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/non-compete-clause-indian-contract-act/)
  - Courts "consistently held that the restrictive covenants that employers impose during employment do not amount to restraint of trade." — [King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/md/labour-employment/restrictive-covenants-employment-contracts-india/)
  - The Supreme Court in *Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning* addressed exclusive-service (negative) covenants during employment and upheld them. — [Ahlawat & Associates](https://www.ahlawatassociates.com/blog/the-legality-of-moonlighting)
  - Commentary lists what *is* enforceable: confidentiality, IP assignment, non-solicitation, and **exclusivity during employment**. — [CalcGuru summary](https://calcguru.in/?p=4647); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/view-point/restrictive-covenants-in-india-evolving-trends-in-employment-law)
  - Delhi High Court reasoning (as summarized): a clause restraining an employee from joining a client post-employment is void unless it protects trade secrets or applies during the term. — [RAW Law](https://rawlaw.in/delhi-high-court-strikes-down-injunction-preventing-ex-employee-from-joining-government-project-post-term-employment-restrictions-are-void-under-indian-law-non-compete-cla/?amp=1)
- **Copyright ownership:**
  - Copyright Act, 1957, Section 17(c): first ownership vests in the employer for works made "in the course of employment under a contract of service," **subject to any agreement to the contrary**.
  - Courts require a contract of service *and* a course-of-employment nexus. Hours alone don't settle it; the question is whether the work falls within the job.
  - The default does not reach work created before joining, or work by contractors, who keep copyright unless assigned under Section 19.
  - — [King Stubb & Kasiva, IP assignment clauses](https://ksandk.com/intellectual-property-rights/ip-assignment-clauses-india/); [Wisemonk](https://www.wisemonk.io/blogs/protect-intellectual-property-hiring-india); [Khurana & Khurana, remote-work copyright](https://www.khuranaandkhurana.com/who-owns-the-home-office-copyright-and-contractual-control-in-remote-work)
- **Patents:** India's Patents Act has no provision deeming an employer to own an employee's invention. Rights stay with the inventor unless assigned in writing, which is why employers rely on contractual assignment clauses. — [Gratanet](https://gratanet.com/publications/rights-to-service-related-works-and-inventions-in-india?region=12); [King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/intellectual-property-rights/ip-assignment-clauses-india/)
- Khaitan & Co's employment-IP Q&A appears to address work created outside working hours (snippet cut off). — [Khaitan & Co Q&A (2021)](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2021-07/Intellectual%20property%20clause%20(employment)%20QAndA%20India.pdf)
- From the employer side, Indian firms advise adding explicit "Conflict of Interest" and "Moonlighting" clauses, so newer contracts are likely to contain them. — [Corrida Legal](https://corridalegal.com/?p=2961); [AZB & Partners (Jul 27, 2023)](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/moonlighting-a-question-of-ethics-or-the-future-of-work/); [Nishith Desai (media quote)](https://nishithdesai.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdfs/NDA%20In%20The%20Media/Quotes/India%E2%80%99s-tech-outsourcing-giants-are-nothappy-about-employees-taking-upsecond-jobs.-Here%E2%80%99s-what-the-law-says.pdf)
- Shot Pattern shows another contract trap: an acquirer's offer came with a 2-year non-compete on golf apps. Acquisition-side non-competes are a separate issue from employment ones. — [The Wedge](https://thewedgegolf.substack.com/p/interview-eric-duffett-shot-pattern-app)

### Inferences
- **Practical checklist for this founder** (synthesized; not legal advice):
  1. Read the employment contract and handbook for exclusivity, outside-business, conflict-of-interest and IP-assignment clauses. In particular, check whether IP assignment covers inventions "whether or not during working hours" or "related to the employer's business."
  2. Prefer written disclosure, or a no-objection letter that names the side project and carves its IP out.
  3. Never use employer laptops, accounts, paid tools, data or work hours.
  4. Avoid any idea that overlaps the employer's business or customers. If the employer is in fintech, SMB software or AI assistants, Munshi or Sarthi could be "related."
  5. Consider incorporating (e.g., an LLP or private limited company) only once it is cleared, because being a director or partner may itself breach a "no other business" clause.
- Section 27 protects the founder *after* leaving, but not *during* employment. The realistic downside of a breach is termination, plus any dispute over IP created with employer resources.
- Running **two** ventures doubles the disclosure surface and the chance that one of them overlaps the employer's domain.

### Gaps
- Retrieved results did not specify how Indian courts treat broadly drafted IP-assignment clauses claiming *unrelated* side projects built on personal time. The Khaitan Q&A that seems to address this could not be read.
- I found no 2025–2026 Indian judgment or statute change specific to moonlighting. Sources are mostly 2022–2023 commentary. The new Labour Codes' treatment of dual employment was not researched.
- Non-Indian employers (e.g., a US company employing the founder in India) may add foreign-law IP clauses. This was not researched.

## Q6. Burnout and sustainability for part-time founders

### Takeaway
Rigorous, part-time-founder-specific burnout data is essentially absent. The earlier notes found no reliable solo-founder burnout statistic, and the 52–54% figures circulating online are untraceable. Practitioner advice is consistent:
- treat 5–10 focused hours/week as realistic;
- use fixed protected blocks rather than leftover energy;
- avoid self-imposed deadline pressure;
- protect sleep;
- watch for slipping day-job performance.

Exemplars accept trade-offs for sustainability (Marc Lou took ~20% less revenue in 2025 for balance). The structural answer from the evidence: a hybrid path lowers failure risk (Raffiee & Feng), and **one** product at a time limits the cognitive load that Paul Graham's "top idea" essay warns about.

### Cited Findings
- **Hours people actually sustain:** one guide says you have "maybe 5–10 hours per week for a side project if you're lucky." Another argues 3–4 focused hours/week can sustain a side income, and that more means "borrowing energy from next month's you." Forum founders report schedules from four nights a week to weekends only. — [Salfbase (Feb 10, 2026)](https://salfbase.pages.dev/blog/2026-02-10-build-side-project-without-burnout/); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/apaksh/how-to-build-a-side-income-as-a-developer-without-burning-out-1id6); [Product Hunt discussion](https://www.producthunt.com/discussions/founders-co-founders-with-a-day-job-how-do-you-manage-work-balance-between-your-two-jobs)
- **Structure over volume:**
  - Reserve one real block instead of relying on leftover energy, and set specific "work nights" and "you nights."
  - Don't pile self-imposed deadlines onto leftover hours.
  - Warning signs are resentment when opening the repo, and slipping day-job quality.
  - Take a couple of weeks off when burnt out, and keep 7+ hours of sleep.
  - "You don't need more time, you need more focus."
  - Sources are blogs and forums. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/working-on-your-startup-while-also-having-a-9-to-5-you-dont-need-more-time-you-need-more-focus-ecf522c523); [Entrepreneur, "How to avoid side hustle burnout"](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/227867); [Echai Ventures Q&A](https://echai.ventures/startingup/productivity-time/how-do-i-protect-my-building-time-when-i-still-have-a-full-time-job-and-i-m-doing-this-on-nights-and-weekends); [Reddit r/SideProject thread](https://lr.us.psf.lt/r/SideProject/comments/1dticmc/i_think_im_burning_out_how_do_you_handle_a_95)

### Inferences
- **A sustainable budget for this founder** is probably ~10–12 focused hours/week in 2–3 fixed blocks, for example two weeknight blocks plus one weekend block, with one fully off day. Plan for occasional zero weeks during day-job crunches. Over 26 weeks, that is ~250–300 real hours. Splitting it across two products leaves each with too little continuity to build momentum.
- The long timelines (Slopes 9 years, Shot Pattern multi-year) mean the plan must be **survivable for years**. That favours one product, low support load, and asynchronous distribution. It counts against a high-touch B2B product unless the founder has strong shop access and enjoys field work.
- Munshi's field-sales and support model (shop visits, WhatsApp support during business hours) carries a higher risk of colliding with the day job than Sarthi's content-and-app model. Sarthi, though, carries a higher risk of slow, demoralizing consumer metrics. Which burnout mode is worse depends on the founder's temperament and access. This is a judgment call; no source settles it.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed or survey data specifically on burnout among part-time or hybrid founders.
- No source quantifies context-switching costs for solo founders running two products. The "context switching" claims are practitioner opinion.
- Hours per week for the cited side-project successes (Shot Pattern, Slopes, ProjectionLab) are undisclosed, so I could not build an evidence-based "hours needed" benchmark.
