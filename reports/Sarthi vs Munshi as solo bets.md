# Bet on Sarthi; make Munshi prove itself

For a founder with 10–15 hours a week, building Sarthi and Munshi in parallel turns two part-time products into two quarter-time ones, and the evidence favours making **Sarthi the only build project for the next 12 weeks**, while Munshi gets a capped, code-free validation sprint and is parked unless strangers pay. Sarthi does not win because its market is kind. Only about 5% of Health & Fitness subscription apps reach even $10K in total revenue in their first two years, AI apps churn about 30% faster on annual plans, and ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini now give memory away. It wins because its distribution (short video and app stores), its low support load and its USD pricing fit evenings and weekends, because the category demonstrably pays (Life Reset, made by two indie developers, is estimated at about $200K a month), and because the founder is its first user. Munshi's best market wedge is an approval-gated agent that chases receivables and flags money leaks on top of the Tally books that ₹1–10 Cr businesses already keep, sold through chartered accountants (CAs). That wedge has better retention economics, but it needs daytime relationship selling and WhatsApp support at ₹500–1,500 a month, and Munshi's 95,000-line codebase was built for a different, weaker wedge (the kirana counter, where ledgers and reminders are free), with no importer for Tally or Vyapar data. **Neither product has had a single real user.** Sarthi's voice input returns the same demo sentence whatever is said, and its memory has no write path; Munshi runs on synthetic shops and cannot bill anyone. The planned memory restart is worth doing as a two-plane design: SQL over the structured logs plus a time-stamped fact store, an always-loaded profile the user can edit, a confirm gate for anything implied or sensitive, nightly consolidation and an owned evaluation suite. That raises the ceiling on retention, not the number of people who find the app, and it must be built to resist memory-driven sycophancy. One fact would flip this recommendation: warm access to CAs, Tally partners or a cluster of traders, combined with enjoying shop visits.

## Sarthi sells into a paying category; Munshi sells against free

### Life Reset proves consumers pay for a structured reset

Life Reset (formerly "Rise: 66 Days Life Reset") is the clearest evidence that Sarthi's category pays. Its publisher, Savvy Tech Limited, appears to be **two indie developers from Hong Kong who launched in March 2024** ([BuildWith](https://buildwith.app/apps/rise)). Airbridge's model estimates **about $203K a month in revenue on about 71K monthly downloads** around October 2026, up from $16K a month 290 days earlier, alongside 407 Meta ads ([Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/app-revenue/life-reset)). Revenue grew about 12× while downloads grew 2.2×, which points to paywall and pricing work plus paid social, not an organic surge. This rests on one tracker's model, and the company's claim of 1.5M downloads has no independent confirmation. The product is narrow. An onboarding quiz builds a single 66-day plan of daily quests for XP and streaks across sleep, water, exercise, mind, screen time and cold showers ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/life-reset-66-day-habit/id6478942469)). No source shows a 33- or 99-day program, so the "33/66/99-day arc" in the founder's notes is the founder's own design, not Life Reset's. The funnel is a long quiz followed by a hard paywall: a yearly plan with a seven-day trial, priced at roughly $12.99 a month or $28.99–59.99 a year ([Operator Standard Review](https://operatorstandardreview.com/life-reset-app-review)). That funnel shows up in the reviews: in one 200-review sample, **34.5% mentioned price, trial or subscription, mostly negatively** ([Operator Standard Review](https://operatorstandardreview.com/life-reset-app-review)). The other recurring complaints are lost progress, billing after cancellation, a skip button that does not break the streak, and shallow game depth ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/6478942469/rise-life-reset-in-66-days/reviews)). Those are complaints about trust and accountability, which is exactly where a coach grounded in real logs could stand apart.

Health & Fitness monetises well as a category and harshly for any single app. RevenueCat's 2026 data puts the category's median trial-to-paid conversion at 37.7% ([Sahha](https://sahha.ai/blog/health-app-monetization-first-month/)) and its **revenue per install at $0.48 by day 14 and $0.66 by day 60, against $0.31 across all categories** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). Annual plans carry 60.6% of the category's revenue, and hard paywalls convert about five times better than freemium (10.7% vs 2.1% of downloads paying by day 35) ([Medium summary of RevenueCat and Adapty](https://chuvak-pavel.medium.com/state-of-app-monetization-2026-key-trends-from-revenuecat-and-adapty-8bdc23a4bb6f)). AI apps earn 41% more per payer but churn 30% faster on annual plans, keeping 21.1% of annual subscribers at 12 months against 30.7% for non-AI apps ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows)). Raw day-30 retention in fitness apps is about 3.7% ([Fitness Refined](https://fitnessrefined.co/fitness-app-retention-statistics/)). Geography matters more than any feature. In India and Southeast Asia, median day-60 revenue per install is $0.11, and realised annual value per payer is **$14 against $23 globally** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). Sarthi's own planned Pro tier, ₹499 a year with "no v1 feature gate" ([DECISIONS.md](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/docs/product/DECISIONS.md)), sits below even that regional median and gives buyers nothing extra for paying. On those revenue-per-install figures, $3K a month needs roughly 4,600 US-weighted installs a month, or about 27,000 India-weighted ones. India's best consumer-subscription precedent shows the cost of the second path: Seekho priced at ₹199 a year (later ₹599) and reached ₹141.5 Cr in FY25 revenue by **spending ₹134 Cr on marketing** ([TheStartupTrends](https://thestartuptrends.com/Blog/7437/Seekho-reports-Rs-142-Cr-revenue-in-FY25-spent-over-Rs-134-crore-on-marketing.aspx)). A bootstrapped founder cannot copy that. Billing friction, at least, has eased: UPI Autopay now works for subscriptions on both Google Play ([GSMArena](https://m.gsmarena.com/newscomm-56538.php)) and the App Store ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108110)).

Competition presses from two sides. AI journals with memory are moving memory and voice behind the paywall. Rosebud, which raised about $6M, set its free plan to end on 30 September 2026, and its paid tier bundles AI reflections, long-term memory and voice ([Mindsera](https://mindsera.com/articles/the-7-best-ai-journaling-apps-in-2026-tested)). layers ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/layers-life-coach-ai-journal/id6504188329)) and Dayora ([Dayora](https://www.dayora.ai/)) sell the same pairing. That validates willingness to pay for memory and also makes it table stakes. General assistants are bundling the rest. OpenAI launched **ChatGPT Health on 7 January 2026**, with Apple Health and MyFitnessPal connectors and a separate health memory ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2026/01/07/openai-launches-chatgpt-health-in-a-push-to-become-a-hub-for-personal-health-data)). ChatGPT's improved memory began reaching free users in June 2026 ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/04/openai-says-chatgpts-memory-feature-is-getting-smarter-and-coming-to-free-users/)), and Claude and Gemini reportedly extended memory to free tiers in early 2026 ([fwdslash](https://www.fwdslash.ai/blog/what-ai-chatbot-has-the-best-memory)). Small teams win in this category: Finch is bootstrapped and was estimated at about $1M a month in June 2026 ([Sensor Tower](https://app.sensortower.com/overview/1528595748?country=US)), and Opal reports $10M ARR with 11 people ([Speedinvest](https://www.speedinvest.com/knowledge/scaling-smart-how-opal-built-a-10m-arr-business-in-just-2-years)). But the solo base rate is low. Habit Pixel reached $1K MRR eight months after launch ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/from-0-to-1k-mrr-in-8-months-bootstrapping-habit-pixel-as-a-solo-dev-684b6c056d)), HabitKit took about 2.5 years to reach $10K MRR ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/602k-revenue-solo-indie-hacker-app-portfolio-breakdown-2026)), and **only about 5% of Health & Fitness apps reach $10K in total revenue within two years** ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-apps-revenue-developers/)). Across all categories, 4.6% of new subscription apps reach $10K in *monthly* revenue within two years ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). No app found in this research logs health, money, habits and skills from one sentence. That gap is either white space or a sign of no demand, since expense tracking is usually its own category, and nothing in the evidence settles which. A realistic year-one outcome for a part-time founder growing organically is $1K–5K MRR, and only if the product converts at category medians. That figure is an estimate built from these benchmarks.

### Indian shops pay for compliance, devices and cash, not ledgers

Recording udhaar (shop credit) and sending reminders did not become a business even for well-funded players. Khatabook raised about $186.5M ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/khatabook)) and reported ₹102.7 Cr of FY24 revenue ([YourStory](https://cwv.yourstory.com/2024/11/ms-dhoni-backed-khatabook-clocks-rs-1027-cr-revenue-cuts-losses-7-in-fy24)); its FY25 figures are unresolved, with one data page showing a collapse to ₹1.4 Cr ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/khatabook/financials/)). OkCredit lost ₹23.2 Cr in FY25 on roughly ₹23–25 Cr of revenue ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/tiger-global-backed-okcredit-narrows-fy25-loss-by-34-to-inr-23-cr/)). Vyapar, the strongest subscription seller, booked ₹69 Cr of FY25 revenue but **spent ₹2.04 for every ₹1 earned and saw its cash fall 93% to ₹6 Cr** ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/vyapar-posts-rs-63-cr-loss-in-fy25-cash-reserve-fades-93-10819211)). The money in this segment goes to payments companies, which give the khata away and charge for devices and loans. Paytm had 1.51 Cr subscription merchants at Q4 FY26 ([Paytm IR](https://paytm.com/blog/investor-relations/key-takeaways-from-our-q4fy26-earnings-call/)) and BharatPe earned ₹1,667 Cr in FY25 ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/bharatpe-turns-ebitda-profitable-in-fy25-revenue-touches-rs-1667-cr-10501335)). It also goes to channel-led incumbents: Tally reported about ₹852 Cr of unaudited FY26 revenue from more than 7M customers ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/tally-solutions-expects-to-cross-1000-cr-revenue-in-fy27-with-25-30-pc-growth)).

The price points show what shops will pay for. Vyapar's mobile plan costs about ₹699–799 a year ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/180579/Vyapar/pricing/)) and myBillBook ₹3,490–3,990 a year ([Accountune](https://accountune.com/mybillbook-alternatives)). TallyPrime Silver costs ₹22,500 once plus ₹4,500 a year ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/business-guides/tallyprime-total-cost-of-ownership-india/)), and a PhonePe soundbox ₹125 a month ([PhonePe](https://cms.phonepe.com/en/mx/merchant-help/phonepe-smartspeaker/about-phonepe-smartspeaker/what-are-charges-applicable-phonepe-smartspeaker)). In the one disclosed datapoint, around 2020, only about **45,000 of Vyapar's 350,000 active users paid** ([Livemint via IndiaMART](https://corporate.indiamart.com/2020/07/27/an-app-to-turn-the-khata-digital-for-small-businesses-livemint)). Kiranas issue 30–80 invoices a month, below the volume where paid billing earns its keep ([Swipe](https://getswipe.in/blog/collections/affordable-gst-billing-software-small-retail-india)). Automated udhaar reminders with payment links are free in OkCredit ([OkCredit](https://okcredit.in/payment-reminder-app)), Khatabook ([Khatabook help](https://sites.google.com/khatabook.com/bizahelpportal/auto-reminder)) and Paytm Business Khata ([Paytm](https://paytm.business/business-khata)). Shops pay for GST compliance, multi-user desktop access, and devices that protect incoming cash. The value anchor above that is human labour: a CA-managed AI bookkeeping service starts at ₹4,999 a month ([AI Accountant](https://www.aiaccountant.com/new-home-page)), and Tally add-ons such as TallyNexa dashboards sell for ₹5,000 a year ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/companies/tallynexa)).

The incumbents with data are adding AI now. Paytm launched an "AI Soundbox" for merchants in October 2025, a conversational assistant in 11–12 Indian languages ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/tech-today/launch/story/paytm-unveils-indias-first-ai-soundbox-for-merchants-shares-jump-25-after-launch-497139-2025-10-07)), and plans to charge subscription and "inference" fees for it ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/11/paytms-q2-ai-soundbox-cautious-postpaid-revival-bnpl-lending)). TallyPrime 7.1 shipped TallyIra, which turns documents into vouchers, on 27 June 2026 ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/darpan/product-update-tallyprime-7-1/)). Tally still describes agentic receivables follow-up as a *future* direction ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/accounting/future-of-ai-in-accounting-and-finance-trends-businesses-should-watch/)), and that gap is Munshi's opening. Abroad, Digits launched "Agentic Close" in June 2026 ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/08/3308040/0/en/digits-launches-agentic-close-turning-the-ledger-into-a-continuous-close-system.html)). Bench shows the opposite path: the bookkeeping startup collapsed in December 2024 because its AI tools "weren't live in time" to support a pared-down human team ([Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/articles/how-a-once-hot-accounting-fintechs-bet-on-ai-led-to-bankruptcy)). The lesson for Munshi is to promise drafts that a named person approves, never finished books or filings, and Munshi's design already does that.

That makes Munshi's winnable wedge narrow. The target is businesses with ₹1–10 Cr turnover that already keep books in Tally or Vyapar and carry B2B credit worth lakhs. Munshi would read their exports, rank who to chase each week, draft WhatsApp reminders with payment links for the owner to approve, and flag leaks such as margin erosion and dead stock, for ₹500–1,500 a month or a share of the cash it recovers. The rails are cheap: WhatsApp utility templates cost about ₹0.115 each ([Blueticks](https://blueticks.co/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026)), so 100 debtors with four reminders a month cost about ₹55, and Razorpay payment links carry about 2% plus GST ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/pricing)). The hard limits keep the bet solo-sized. Munshi must never hold or route funds, which requires a payment-aggregator licence and ₹15 Cr net worth ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/financial-services/1689480/an-analysis-of-the-reserve-bank-of-indias-master-direction-on-regulation-of-payment-aggregators-2025)). It must never arrange loans ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/financial-services/1629916/rbi-digital-lending-directions-2025-an-overview)), and it must never pose as a general-purpose AI chatbot on WhatsApp, which Meta has barred since 15 January 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/18/whatssapp-changes-its-terms-to-bar-general-purpose-chatbots-from-its-platform/)). It must also meet India's data-protection law (DPDP) duties as a processor by 13 May 2027 ([IndiaDataLaw](https://indiadatalaw.org/deadlines/)). This research found no verified solo founder with meaningful revenue in this segment. A realistic year-one outcome through two to five CA or Tally-partner relationships is 50–150 paying businesses, roughly ₹25K–2.25 lakh MRR, an estimate rather than a benchmark.

|---|---|---|

## Neither codebase has met a user, and each skipped its hardest part

The two repositories share a founder, a stack generation (Next.js 16, Supabase Postgres, Vercel) and a philosophy of proposing before writing, but they differ sharply in size and readiness. **Munshi has about 95,000 lines of product code and 65,000 lines of tests across 151 tables.** Its visible history runs from 31 October to 13 November 2026, on top of a large earlier tree, and it served at least three hackathon submissions; its own docs say "This is a hackathon build" ([docs/kit/overview.md](../docs/kit/overview.md)). **Sarthi has about 32,000 lines of product code and 8,000 of tests across 38 tables.** It was built in 5.7 days, 17–22 July 2026, for OpenAI Build Week, and has been idle since ([README.md](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/README.md)). Both repos record zero users. Munshi's limitations page says "no real shop has used Munshi" and all data is synthetic ([docs/limitations.md](../docs/limitations.md)). Sarthi's PRD names the founder as primary user ([PRD.md](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/docs/product/PRD.md)), and nothing in the repo shows the daily dogfooding it promised.

Munshi's engineering is strong where a money product needs it. A database trigger and a write-less agent role mean nothing changes the books without a named person's approval. Row-level security is forced on every tenant table. Per-shop AI spend caps check a deployment switch, shop switch, daily caps and a monthly budget before every model call, and write a spend row afterwards ([ADR 0006](../docs/decisions/0006-per-shop-router-and-spend-caps.md)). A cite-or-abstain validator rejects free numbers in English, Devanagari and romanised Hindi, and the UI ships in English and Hindi ([README.md](../README.md)). What Munshi lacks is everything between a demo and a customer. It has no way to charge a shop and no privacy or terms pages. Onboarding waits for an operator to accept the shop ([ADR 0010](../docs/decisions/0010-real-shop-acceptance.md)), and AI starts switched off for new shops ([ADR 0006](../docs/decisions/0006-per-shop-router-and-spend-caps.md)). WhatsApp stays in recorded mode unless five live conditions hold, and PayPal runs in sandbox only, which is the wrong rail for a UPI-first market ([docs/limitations.md](../docs/limitations.md)).

The deeper problem is aim. Munshi's README describes an owner who is "the cashier, the buyer, the stock-keeper, the credit desk and the payroll office", and the demo runs two kiranas, a salon and a boutique ([README.md](../README.md)). In other words, Munshi is built to be the counter's system of record, which is the segment where the market evidence says ledgers and reminders are free and quick commerce squeezes the customer base ([KNN India](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/sectors/others/distributors-body-claim-2-lakh-kirana-stores-shut-down-because-of-quick-commerce-companies); the closure figure is disputed). The wedge with the best odds sits on top of books that businesses already keep elsewhere. **A search of the Munshi repository finds no importer for TallyPrime, Vyapar or myBillBook data**: its only spreadsheet reader parses payment-settlement statements ([settlements.ts](../apps/web/lib/server/settlements.ts)). The late commits add integrations chosen for hackathon prize tracks (AG Grid, Bryntum, APIMatic, Postman, Zapier and a PayPal desk), not for shop demand. Moving to the right wedge would carry over the approval core (finding, draft, named approval, audit), the number validator, the spend caps and Hindi. Most of the remaining surface, including 151 tables, two shop websites, an operator console and public SDKs, would be code to maintain that no user has asked for.

Sarthi has the opposite profile: a sound core with its defining features faked. Its framework-free `core/` (capture 2,004 lines, onboarding 1,636, domains 1,381, game 489) is boundary-checked for later reuse in a mobile shell ([AGENTS.md](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/AGENTS.md)). Its capture trust boundary is well designed: an entry is written automatically only when it is a non-estimated create at **≥9,000 basis points of confidence** with a resolved entity, everything else becomes a confirm card, and photos never auto-write ([route.ts](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/core/capture/route.ts)). But the headline interaction is a stub. `FakeVoiceProvider.transcribe` ignores the audio and returns the canonical demo sentence, and every non-fake voice option throws ([voice.ts](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/providers/fake/voice.ts)). Production photo storage throws "not configured" ([production.ts](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/providers/media/production.ts)). Billing defaults to waitlist mode with a Razorpay schema and no checkout route ([runtime.ts](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/app/lib/runtime.ts)). Real model calls need the user's own API key, and nothing caps AI spend on the server. Account deletion is unavailable ([SettingsSheet.tsx](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/components/settings/SettingsSheet.tsx)), the export omits coach messages and memory ([export route](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/app/api/settings/export/route.ts)), and tenant isolation lives only in application code, with no row-level security ([scope.ts](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/data/repository/scope.ts)).

| What the first 10 paying users need | Munshi today | Sarthi today |
|---|---|---|
| Core input working live | Yes: live models on the hosted demo, but only synthetic shops | No: voice is fake, photo storage unwired |
| Auth and tenant isolation | Strong: forced RLS, signed sessions, lockouts | Supabase auth built but env-gated; app-level scoping only |
| AI cost control | Per-shop caps, daily ceilings, spend ledger | None server-side; bring-your-own-key |
| Taking money | Nothing built | Schema only; waitlist mode |
| Legal and data rights | No privacy or terms pages | Stub pages; no deletion; partial export |
| Fit to the best wedge | Built for the counter; no Tally/Vyapar import | Four domains at once; no narrow wedge |
| Surface for one part-timer to maintain | ≈160K lines incl. tests, 151 tables | ≈40K lines incl. tests, 38 tables |

Both projects spent their effort on proof and process rather than learning. Munshi runs 1,916 "red-proof" sabotage cases and has 12 architecture decision records; Sarthi carries 13,386 lines of process and planning markdown (about 41% of its product-code line count), signed plans and a nine-agent Codex setup. Yet each product's defining capability, a shop's real books for Munshi and real voice plus durable memory for Sarthi, is absent. The 5.7-day Sarthi build shows that agent-assisted coding has stopped being the bottleneck, which echoes Rob Walling's 2026 episode title "Building Is Not the Hard Part" ([Startups for the Rest of Us](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-845-lifetime-deals-revisited-building-is-not-the-hard-part-and-confirming-an-idea-is-worth-paying-for-rob-solo)). In the next quarter, every build hour should pay for a test with users.

## Two part-time products means two quarter-time products

The arithmetic is blunt. At 10–15 hours a week, a quarter holds **130–195 hours**; split two ways, each product gets 65–100 hours, about two full-time weeks, before any cost of switching between them. MicroConf's survey finds full-time founders grow **2.2× faster** than part-time ones ([MicroConf](https://microconf.com/founders/0-10k-arr)); if hours drive even part of that gap, halving them again compounds the penalty. Practitioners who advise bootstrappers converge on focus. Rob Walling calls "don't launch a second product" the default stance ([Startups for the Rest of Us](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-681-why-launching-a-second-product-is-usually-a-bad-idea)), Paul Graham writes that "only one idea at a time can be the top idea in your mind" ([paulgraham.com](https://paulgraham.com/top.html)), and Justin Jackson's existing business "made way less revenue" once his attention moved to Transistor ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/founder-interviews-justin-jackson-of-transistor-fm-1e9003e27cd)).

The portfolio builders are not a counterexample once their conditions are examined. Pieter Levels says 4 of his 70-plus projects made money, a 5% hit rate ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/discussions/only-4-out-of-70-projects-i-ever-did-made-money-and-grew)), but he shipped them in sequence to an audience he already had. Tony Dinh keeps only one SaaS and makes his other products low-maintenance one-time purchases ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/heres-how-these-founders-are-building-multiple-products-at-once-f4ee0aeb98)). Daniel Vassallo's small bets were info products sold to a large Twitter following ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/general/i-m-daniel-i-made-404-473-selling-an-ebook-and-a-video-course-on-the-internet-ama)). Portfolios work when the products share an audience or a codebase, need little support, or ride existing distribution ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/the-unsexy-truth-about-running-a-portfolio-of-ai-micro-products-b5d8508234)). None of those conditions holds here. Sarthi's buyers are on Reels and app stores, Munshi's are in shops and CA offices, and only the LLM plumbing carries over. None of the side-project successes in the evidence ran two products during the side-project phase either: Shot Pattern, Slopes, ProjectionLab and Carrd were each one product. The day job itself is an asset. Hybrid entrepreneurs who keep their jobs are about **33% less likely to fail** ([Inc. on Raffiee & Feng](https://www.inc.com/jessica-stillman/should-you-quit-your-day-job-study-says-no.html)). Eric Duffett built Shot Pattern to $185K in 2024 and $1M in lifetime sales while still teaching full-time ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/eric-duffett-shot-pattern-launched-podcast-2026)), and ProjectionLab's founder quit only at about $23.3K MRR ([ProjectionLab](https://projectionlab.com/blog/quit-my-day-job)). What the evidence supports is **parallel validation and serial building**: cheap, time-boxed demand tests on both ideas, then nearly all build hours on the winner.

To choose the winner, the table below scores both ideas on criteria drawn from YC, Paul Graham, Justin Jackson and Tony Dinh. It doubles the weight on the two criteria that consume a part-timer's scarcest resource, hours: whether the founder can reach buyers, and how much support each customer needs. The scores are this report's judgement on the cited evidence, not a published model.

| Criterion (weight) | Sarthi | Munshi | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder–market fit (1×) | 4 | 2 | Founder is Sarthi's intended user; no evidence of shop access |
| Distribution reachable at 10–15 h/week (2×) | 3 | 2 | Asynchronous content vs evening and weekend visits to CAs and owners |
| Evidence of existing spend (1×) | 4 | 2 | Life Reset, Finch, Opal vs free ledgers and ₹700/yr billing |
| Time to first dollar (1×) | 3 | 3 | Pre-orders vs a paid concierge pilot; neither has billing |
| Support load per customer (2×) | 4 | 2 | Self-serve app vs WhatsApp support in business hours |
| ARPU × reachable volume (1×) | 3 | 2 | $30–60/yr globally vs ₹500–1,500/mo from tens of shops |
| Platform and incumbent risk (1×) | 2 | 2 | Free assistant memory vs Paytm and Tally AI |
| Retention ceiling (1×) | 2 | 4 | ≈3.7% day-30 consumer vs ≈60% six-month SMB |
| Founder energy (1×) | 4 | 3 | Founder wants to restart Sarthi |
| **Weighted total** | **36** | **26** | |

The scores show the two ideas' strengths are mirror images. Sarthi has low support and high churn; Munshi has low churn and high support. For someone with a day job, churn is a metrics problem that can be worked on at night, while support load is a calendar problem that collides with work hours. The record of solo founders who passed $1M points the same way: the ones who stayed solo sold licences, ads or app-store software that create little support per dollar, while those selling support-heavy products hired, as GMass did on its way to about 11 people ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/gmass)). Munshi creates a lot of support per rupee. Sarthi is the less-bad bet, not a safe one, and its platform and retention risks are real. The flip condition is specific. If the founder has warm access to CAs, Tally partners or a cluster of ₹1–10 Cr traders, and enjoys visiting them, Munshi's fit and distribution scores rise to 4–5 and the totals come close to even. At that point the higher SMB retention ceiling, about 60% versus 40% "good" six-month retention for consumer SaaS ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-retention-issue-29)), makes Munshi the stronger long-run business.

One check comes before either test, and it is legal rather than strategic. India has no general moonlighting statute, so the employment contract governs. Courts void non-competes after employment ends but uphold exclusivity *during* employment ([King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/md/labour-employment/restrictive-covenants-employment-contracts-india/)), and Wipro fired about 300 employees for moonlighting in 2022 ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/what-is-moonlighting-here-s-why-wipro-fired-300-employees-for-doing-it-news-225205)). Under Section 17(c) of the Copyright Act, the employer owns works made "in the course of employment" unless the contract says otherwise ([King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/intellectual-property-rights/ip-assignment-clauses-india/)). The founder should read the exclusivity and IP-assignment clauses, get a written no-objection that names the project and carves out its IP, and never use employer devices, accounts or hours. Running two ventures doubles the disclosure surface and the chance that one overlaps the employer's business.

### Cheap tests that fit evenings and weekends

Sarthi's test is content-first and asynchronous, which suits a day job. PushScroll's founders posted a fake product-demo video that drew about 80K views and about 500 requests to build the app ([Starter Story recap](https://onepage-research.sliplane.app/articles/youtube.com/yt-ST2ROU-0932ccd0)). Views show interest, not willingness to pay ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/general/what-are-the-strongest-signals-that-validate-an-idea-and-show-that-the-product-is-worth-building)), so the test should end in a priced annual pre-order. The content should also test one narrow frame instead of four domains at once. One option is a 66-day reset in which you say your day out loud, with one hook aimed at Indian young professionals and one at a global English-speaking audience, to see which converts at which price. A 20–50 person beta then supplies the real signal: Sean Ellis's survey, where at least 40% of users "very disappointed" to lose the product marks fit and Superhuman started at 22% ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)), plus week-4 logging retention and paying users. Once billing exists, the salary can fund a capped Meta ad test of $200–300 to measure cost per install and trial conversion, the levers behind Life Reset's growth. At a cost per install near $1, a US-weighted Health & Fitness app roughly breaks even on 60-day revenue; that is arithmetic on RevenueCat's medians, not a published benchmark.

Munshi's test needs no new code. OkCredit's founders walked into Bengaluru shops and later posted how-to videos in WhatsApp groups ([KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/indian-mom-and-pop-stores-use-okcredit-to-digitize-sales-records-startup-stories)). Instamojo found that micro-merchants pay when software touches incoming money, after three pricing revisions in twelve months; its CEO said "we never thought merchants were going to pay by their own accord" ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/5d2cfb77-4c2d-49e8-b968-5428e29d4614/)). The cheapest Munshi test is 10–20 evening or weekend conversations: three to five with CAs or Tally partners, and the rest with owners of ₹1–10 Cr businesses that extend B2B credit. With consent, the founder then takes one Tally export and delivers a hand-assembled report of who owes money, who to chase first and what leaked, with draft WhatsApp messages, and asks for a paid pilot.

| | Sarthi | Munshi |
|---|---|---|
| Test | Content hooks → waitlist with priced pre-order → 20–50 person beta | 10–20 conversations → 1–3 concierge reports from real Tally exports → paid pilot ask |
| Hours | ≈1–2 h/week content on top of the build | ≈3 h/week for six weeks, then zero |
| Continue if | Founder logs ≥5 days/week; ≥200 stranger sign-ups and ≥10 paid pre-orders by week 6; Ellis ≥40% by week 12 (25–40% = iterate once) | ≥3 businesses pay or sign a paid pilot, and ≥1 CA or partner offers referrals, by week 6 |

## Sarthi's memory should store evidence, not impressions

### The current coach reads a memory it never writes

Sarthi's coach has a "two-layer memory". The first layer is the last 12 coach messages. The second is a `coach_memory` table whose content is immutable, ranked at read time by a frecency score (0.6 × recency with a 60-day decay plus 0.4 × log-frequency), with pinned rows always included, the top eight per detected domain, and an append-only audit table ([memory.ts](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/core/coach/memory.ts)). The retrieval half is built and tested; the write half does not exist. No production path creates a memory. The only rows come from the demo seed's two pinned goals ([demo.ts](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/data/seed/demo.ts)), and the `/api/coach/memory` route that the UI posts to was never written ([CoachReadingRoom.tsx](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/components/coach/CoachReadingRoom.tsx)). **A user who taps "Start fresh" has an empty durable memory forever**, so the README's claim that the coach "brings those up across conversations" holds only for the demo persona.

The read side has defects that a restart should not inherit. Usage is counted when a memory is *injected* into the prompt, not when the reply *uses* it. Every row that reaches the top eight therefore refreshes its own recency and frequency: a row used ten times and surfaced today scores about 0.88, while a brand-new memory scores 0.60, so incumbents monopolise the slots. Domain detection is a keyword regex. Memories are not fenced as untrusted text in the prompt envelope, even though questions, transcripts and tool logs are. The engine computes whether citations are valid and then ignores the result, and numbers in the coach's prose are never checked ([agent.ts](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/core/coach/agent.ts)). Memory reaches only the conversation; daily briefs and weekly reflections never read it ([engine.ts](https://github.com/SawhneySatvik/Sarthi/blob/main/core/coach/engine.ts)). The coach and memory code together are about 2,500 lines, roughly 8% of Sarthi's product code, so memory is the smaller part of any restart.

### The 2026 pattern: two planes, a confirm gate and a nightly pass

The field has converged on a small always-loaded profile, searchable stores, a write pipeline that extracts and reconciles facts, and background consolidation. Mem0's design extracts candidate facts from each exchange, retrieves the ten most similar existing memories, and has an LLM choose ADD, UPDATE, DELETE or NOOP ([arXiv 2504.19413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413)). In April 2026 Mem0 moved to a cheaper single-pass, add-only extraction call ([Mem0](https://mem0.ai/blog/mem0-the-token-efficient-memory-algorithm)). Zep's Graphiti tracks when a fact was true separately from when the system learned it, and marks contradicted facts invalid instead of deleting them ([Zep paper notes](https://github.com/AviAvni/database-learning-path/blob/master/topics/38-graphrag-agent-memory/reading-zep-graphiti.md)). Letta showed that simple search beats elaborate structure: an agent with only grep and file-search tools over plain files scored **74.0% on the LoCoMo memory benchmark with GPT-4o-mini, above Mem0's best reported 68.5%** ([Letta](https://www.letta.com/blog/benchmarking-ai-agent-memory)). Consumer products consolidate offline; Claude's consumer memory processes updates once a day ([Anthropic](https://support.anthropic.com/en/articles/11817273-how-does-claude-s-memory-work)).

Long context does not replace memory. On the LongMemEval benchmark, Zep's retrieval scored 71.2% against 60.2% for feeding the full history, at 2.58 seconds against 28.9 ([memorypapers.org](https://memorypapers.org/papers/zep-a-temporal-knowledge-graph-architecture-for-agent-memory)), and the BEAM benchmark finds that even million-token models struggle as dialogues lengthen ([arXiv 2510.27246](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.27246)). A daily Sarthi user producing 2–4K tokens a day accumulates roughly 0.7–1.5M tokens a year, so replaying the full history stops working within months. The hard operations are updating and forgetting. The HaluMem benchmark measured **update accuracy below 26% on medium-length histories and near zero on long ones** ([HaluMem](https://huggingface.co/papers/2511.03506)), and selective forgetting across several hops scored as low as 7% on MemoryAgentBench ([Liner review](https://liner.com/review/evaluating-memory-in-llm-agents-via-incremental-multiturn-interactions)). Memory also makes models more agreeable. User-memory profiles raised agreement sycophancy by **45% for Gemini 2.5 Pro, 33% for Claude Sonnet 4 and 16% for GPT-4.1 Mini** ([arXiv 2509.12517](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12517)), and WRITER found that replacing extracted memory snippets with raw chat history roughly halved sycophancy ([WRITER](https://writer.com/engineering/personalized-context-degrades-ai-accuracy/)). That last point matters for a coach whose job is to say "your logs disagree".

For Sarthi, those findings point to two planes in one Postgres database: structured data the coach queries, and narrative memory that holds the reasons behind it.

| Component | What it holds | Key design choice | Borrowed from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structured plane | Food, spend, habit and skill-hour logs; daily and weekly rollups; goals and commitments as rows with metric, target, cadence and deadline | The coach answers number questions with a whitelisted `query_logs` tool; "did I keep my commitment?" becomes a SQL join, not a recall guess | Sarthi's typed schema |
| Profile block | ≈800–1,500 tokens: context, constraints, preferences, coaching style, a do-not-mention list | Always in the prompt; user-editable; regenerated nightly, with sections the user locked winning | Letta memory blocks |
| `memory_facts` | Atomic, dated, third-person statements with a slot key (e.g. `finance.monthly_budget`), kind, source, sensitivity, evidence and confidence | Bi-temporal (`valid_from`/`valid_to` plus recorded/superseded times); superseded, never overwritten | Zep; Mem0 |
| `sessions` | One summary and its key events per conversation | Summarised nightly; searchable | Generative Agents; MemoryBank |
| Proposals, audit, consents | Pending confirm cards; every memory operation; per-domain consent | Implied, inferred or sensitive facts wait for the user; audit keeps IDs, not content, after erasure | Sarthi's confirm rule; DPDP |

The **write path** runs asynchronously after each turn and never blocks the reply. A cheap model extracts candidates, each tagged as stated or implied, with a sensitivity level and an evidence quote. A policy gate then auto-saves only stated, non-sensitive facts. It sends implied or inferred ones to a confirm card, and either drops sensitive ones that lack consent for their domain or asks for that consent. Reconciliation is deterministic wherever possible. A candidate whose slot key matches an active fact either confirms it (same value) or supersedes it, setting the old row's `valid_to`. Only facts without a slot key go to an LLM, which chooses add, update, supersede or no-op against the five to ten most similar facts. This is the design's answer to the update failures HaluMem measures: the commonest changes, such as a budget, a weight goal or a diet, never depend on an LLM's judgement. When a user deletes a fact, its hash joins a do-not-re-extract list, so the extractor cannot relearn it the next day.

The **read path** works under a fixed budget of about 2–4K tokens. Every prompt includes the profile, goals and commitments due soon, a seven- and thirty-day stats card from the rollups, and the last session's summary. Query-specific facts come from hybrid retrieval that fuses Postgres full-text search with vector similarity through reciprocal rank fusion (Supabase documents the pattern ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/ai/hybrid-search))). Results are filtered to facts valid for the time the question asks about, then reranked with frecency, importance and pins as signals. Facts enter the prompt with their date and source ("[14 Sep 2026, you said] …"), which curbs both stale facts and sycophancy. Each user's fact count stays small for months, so a first version can ship with full-text search and slot lookups alone and add embeddings once the eval suite shows keyword recall falling short; that sequencing is this report's judgement, not a published threshold. The model also gets tools: `search_memory`, `get_fact_history`, `query_logs`, `propose_memory` and `forget`. A fact's use count rises only when the reply actually cites it, and the interface shows "used in this reply" chips, following Gemini's practice of showing how a response was personalised ([DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/gemini-personal-intelligence)). A **nightly job** summarises sessions, merges near-duplicates, regenerates the profile, decays unused facts so they retire (still visible) rather than vanish, and expires time-bound facts such as "travelling this week". Once a week it drafts at most three evidence-citing insights as proposals for the user, never as automatic facts, because inferred judgements about a person are the likeliest to feel wrong or creepy.

Build all of this rather than buy it. Mem0's hosted Hobby tier allows 1,000 retrievals a month, too few for a daily coach, and Pro costs $249 a month ([Cognee](https://www.cognee.ai/mem0-pricing)). Graphiti needs Neo4j, FalkorDB, Kuzu or Neptune, not Postgres ([Graphiti docs](https://www.mintlify.com/getzep/graphiti/api/drivers/overview)). Anthropic's memory tool is stable and client-side, but it is tied to Claude ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/memory-tool)), which conflicts with Sarthi's provider-agnostic model layer. Copy its interface (view, search and edit tools with a handler Sarthi owns) instead. Owning the tables makes DPDP erasure, user editing and joins with the logs straightforward.

The suite that proves the system works has to be owned too, because public scores mostly measure the answering model: the same memory system scored 95.4% or 84.23% on LongMemEval depending on which model read the retrieved memories ([OMEGA](https://omegamax.co/blog/number-one-on-longmemeval)). Sarthi needs 20–40 scripted synthetic personas, each with 15–40 sessions over three to twelve simulated months and seeded logs. Write-path scoring covers extraction precision and recall, invented facts, policy violations and update correctness. Read-path questions cover recall, aggregation across sessions, time, updated facts, false premises, abstention, honoured deletions, SQL-checked numbers, leakage between domains, and sycophancy probes where the user claims progress that the logs contradict. Score deterministically wherever possible, and use a judge model from a different family, calibrated on about 100 hand-labelled items, for the rest. For DPDP, which applies in full from 13 May 2027 ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1708164/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified)), health- and finance-adjacent memory needs per-purpose consent, withdrawal as easy as consent, and erasure that cascades to facts, summaries, embeddings, the profile and any evaluation copies.

### Port the discipline, leave the scaffolding

| Keep from current Sarthi (and Munshi) | Replace or avoid |
|---|---|
| The capture trust boundary (auto-write only at ≥9,000 bps, non-estimated, resolved) and the confirm-card rule | Counting use at injection rather than citation |
| Immutable content with a bounded lifecycle; retire, never delete; append-only audit | Regex domain detection; memory reaching only the chat |
| Integer confidence in basis points and the `estimated` flag | Unfenced memory text in the prompt |
| Pinned goals, rebuilt as goal rows plus a ranking boost | Citation checks computed and ignored; unchecked numbers in prose |
| Untrusted-text fencing, citation validation, the keyless fake stack, controlled-clock tests | Listing every memory row and filtering in JavaScript; a single-flight lock per serverless instance |
| Munshi's spend caps (`allow()` plus spend rows) and its number validator for English and Hindi | The dual SQLite/Postgres layer, anonymous sandboxes, bring-your-own-key as the default |
| Munshi's Keeper note discipline: notes cite a decision, expire by default (180 days), can be forgotten with an audit trail, and are findable for erasure ([memory.ts](../packages/core/src/memory.ts)) | Graph databases, hosted memory APIs, provider-locked memory, auto-saved "insights", storing flattering identity statements |

On restart scope, the founder should restart the repository, not the product. The audit's line counts show that keeping the repo and rebuilding only the coach and memory would discard about 2,500 of 32,300 product lines. A fresh Postgres-only repo would port about 6,000 lines of core logic, 1,200 of providers and 9,600 of components and theme, plus the art. **Either route leaves the same unbuilt list: real speech-to-text, photo storage, billing, deletion and the memory write path.** A clean restart therefore buys cleanliness, not progress: one database dialect, row-level security on user-owned tables as defence in depth, and no planning archive to drag along. That is worth having only if the port is capped at about three weeks (roughly 30 hours, this report's estimate). The two Munshi modules should be copied directly rather than extracted into a shared package, because a shared platform is infrastructure work that validates nothing.

### Memory raises retention's ceiling, not the funnel's top

Today Sarthi is a coach that forgets everyone except the demo persona. That disqualifies a product pitched on remembering its user, in a segment where AI apps already lose annual subscribers faster. A proper memory removes that defect and is a precondition for renewals. It does not differentiate on its own: Rosebud charges for memory, and ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini give it away. What a chat assistant lacks, and Sarthi could own, is **memory joined to structured logs**: goals stored as rows, commitments checked against logged evidence, numbers drawn from SQL rather than recollection, and a game loop like Life Reset's on top. The same grounding is the best defence against memory-driven sycophancy, because the coach can say "your spend log disagrees" instead of agreeing. No published study measures how memory design affects retention in AI coaches, so Sarthi's memory is a hypothesis to test in the beta. Track it through the Ellis survey, week-4 logging retention and one direct question: "did the coach remember something that mattered?" Cost has to be designed in. Memory likely adds 20–50% to the cost of a small-model chat (an estimate; no source measured it for a coaching app), and consumer AI apps range from about $0.09 to $18.24 per daily active user per month depending mostly on voice ([Inworld](https://inworld.ai/resources/unit-economics-of-consumer-ai-apps)). At Indian prices that rules out real-time voice and calls for short speech-to-text clips, a cheap parsing model and rare calls to a large model. Net effect: memory improves Sarthi's *conditional* odds, meaning users who arrive stay and renew. It does not improve the *unconditional* odds that anyone arrives. The roughly 5% base rate is a distribution problem, and memory does not solve it.

## Twelve weeks, two gates, one build

**Recommendation:** Sarthi becomes the single build project. Munshi gets at most three hours a week of code-free validation for six weeks, then parks unless strangers pay. Both run inside fixed blocks of about 12 hours a week: two weeknight blocks plus one weekend block, one day fully off, and expected zero weeks during day-job crunches. Practitioners describe that schedule as sustainable, and they treat 5–10 focused hours as realistic for most side projects ([Salfbase](https://salfbase.pages.dev/blog/2026-02-10-build-side-project-without-burnout/)).

| 1–3 | Fresh Postgres-only repo; port capture, domains, game, onboarding, schema, fake stack and art; real speech-to-text on short clips; server-side model keys with per-user daily caps; Supabase auth on, sandboxes off. Landing page and waitlist with a priced annual pre-order; 2–3 short videos a week testing an Indian and a global hook | 5–8 conversations with CAs, Tally partners and owners of ₹1–10 Cr businesses; ask for one Tally export | Founder logging real days by voice; first videos live |
| 4–6 | Memory v1: goals and commitments as rows, `query_logs`, profile block, slot-keyed bi-temporal facts, proposals, audit, consents, full-text search; briefs and reflections read memory; 10-persona eval skeleton; daily dogfooding | 1–3 concierge receivables-and-leak reports with draft WhatsApp reminders; ask for a paid pilot or a share of recovered cash | **Gate A** |

**Gate A, end of week 6.** Money beats interest. Munshi stays alive only if at least three businesses pay or sign a paid pilot and at least one CA or Tally partner offers referrals. If Munshi clears that bar and Sarthi has no paid pre-orders, the build hours swap to Munshi. The wedge then becomes a Tally-export importer feeding the existing approval core, with billing and legal pages, and the counter-POS surface is frozen. If both clear their gates, compare paying strangers. If neither clears, the honest move is to stop writing code and spend four weeks narrowing Sarthi's frame and retesting content. Sarthi's own bar at Gate A is that the founder logs at least five days a week for the last three weeks, because a coach its maker abandons will not hold strangers, plus at least 200 sign-ups from strangers and at least 10 paid pre-orders. **Gate B, end of week 12.** Launch publicly if the Ellis score is at least 40% (25–40% earns one more iteration), at least a third of the beta is still logging in week 4, and at least 10 users pay the intended price. Below 25% with no payers, stop or pivot rather than keep polishing the memory. All of these thresholds are this report's synthesis, calibrated to the benchmarks above.

## Conclusion

Across roughly 130,000 lines of product code in the two repositories, the founder's bottleneck has never been building. Two products built to a high standard of correctness have not yet had one conversation with a paying stranger, and the memory restart risks being the comfortable next step for the same reason the first builds were. The plan above therefore ties every block of code to a test with a user and caps the restart's scope. The less obvious lesson is that the founder's most valuable asset is not either product but a trust architecture both already share. Munshi refuses to state an unsourced number and lets a named person approve every change; Sarthi auto-writes only above a confidence threshold and asks before acting on estimates. That discipline, carried into Sarthi as a memory that cites evidence from logs instead of flattering impressions, is what could separate a part-time founder's coach from the free memory in ChatGPT.

The decision is also more conditional than a ranking suggests. Sarthi leads because a part-timer can absorb churn more easily than support load, not because consumer self-improvement is an easy market. A single fact the research could not observe, whether the founder can walk into CA offices and trader clusters with warm introductions, would reverse it, because a B2B product that people keep paying for compounds in a way a 3.7%-day-30 consumer app rarely does. Running the six-week Munshi probe answers exactly that question for about 18 hours of effort. That is cheap insurance against picking the wrong bet for the next several years.
