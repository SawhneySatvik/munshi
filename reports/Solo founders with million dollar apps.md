# Owned distribution carries solo apps past $1M

As of November 2026, about three dozen people have credible evidence that they built an app essentially alone and took it past $1M in revenue, and they fall into a few repeatable shapes rather than a scatter of lucky hits. **The largest and best-documented outcomes are premium games with official sales counts**: Eric Barone's Stardew Valley had sold over 41 million copies by December 2024 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/stardew-valley-sells-over-41-million-copies)). The strongest software cases come from two eras: Pieter Levels (Nomad List, Remote OK), Mike Perham (Sidekiq), Ivan Kutskir (Photopea), AJ (Carrd) and Jason Zigelbaum (Zigpoll) from the bootstrapped 2010s, and Maor Shlomo (Base44, sold to Wix for $80M in cash about six months after launch), Marc Lou, Tony Dinh, Danny Postma, Nevo David and Josh Mohrer from the AI era. **The revenue evidence behind the software names is thin**: apart from one public-company acquisition, official game sales and a few Stripe-verified listings, nearly every figure comes from the founder, and third-party estimates miss founders' own numbers by 2–7×. What the winners share is not better code but **distribution that existed before or alongside the product** (an audience, a marketplace listing, an open-source funnel, a viral footer, streamers) plus a business model that creates little support work per dollar, which is what lets one person keep up. Classic solo SaaS took four to nine years to reach $1M; AI-era products got there in weeks or months and often faded as fast, with ShipFast down about 98% from its peak and fly.pieter.com reportedly at $0. **The base rate is brutal**: about 1% of startups on the verified-revenue site TrustMRR sit in its $1M+ band, and only 4.6% of new subscription apps reach even $10K a month within two years. The lessons for an aspiring builder are to choose a low-churn, low-support model before choosing an idea, to own at least one channel that compounds, to treat dependence on any single platform as a risk that can end the business, and to read early speed as a cue to deepen the moat or sell rather than as proof the revenue will last.

## About three dozen builders clear the bar, and games dwarf the software

"Solo" is the most stretched word in this genre, so this report applies a strict test: one person did the core design and engineering and was the only full-time worker, or nearly so, when the product crossed $1M in annual revenue. Freelance art, music or support help and a third-party publisher are tolerated but flagged, and where only lifetime revenue exists the report says so. On that test the software list is short, the mobile list is longest in the 2010s App Store era, and premium PC and console games dominate in absolute dollars.

**A web or SaaS product that was genuinely solo at $1M+ is rarer than the indie-hacker feed suggests.** Only six or seven builders from the 2010–2022 era are well documented as solo when they crossed the line, and each sits in one of four archetypes that limit how much support work a dollar of revenue creates. The first is the ad-supported free tool: Photopea earns about 90% of its revenue from ads, and its revenue "has always directly corresponded to the size of the user base" ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-reddit-ama-earned-photopea-12m-monthly-users-7c66c1d9ad)). The second is the open-core developer library, where Sidekiq lists Pro at $99 a month or $995 a year and Enterprise from $269 a month per 100 threads ([sidekiq.org](https://sidekiq.org/)). The third is the marketplace-native app, such as Zigpoll and Björn Forsberg's FORSBERG+two in the Shopify App Store or GMass inside Gmail. The fourth is Levels' paid community and job board, fed by a build-in-public audience. Perham is the strongest case of a solo business above $1M for a decade: he was making **$80,000 a month without a single hire** around 2017 ([Indie Hackers podcast](https://indiehackers.com/podcast/016-mike-perham-of-sidekiq)) and still had no employees at roughly 2,000 customers around 2023 ([Startups for the Rest of Us](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-661-millions-in-revenue-as-a-one-person-software-company)). Forsberg is the portfolio-plus-contractors variant: he paid developers in India about $2,000 for his first app and reached $1M+ ARR across several Shopify apps over roughly eight years ([Bootstrappers](https://bootstrappers.com/wanting-more-time-with-family-this-founder-built-a-suite-of-shopify-apps-that-generate-over-1-million-arr/)), serving 100,000+ merchants ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/news/developer-entrepreneurs)).

**General-purpose SaaS kept strictly solo tends to stall at $0.6–0.9M.** Damon Chen's Testimonial.to was about $840K ARR in late 2023 ([TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2023/11/03/from-4-failures-to-1-million-bootstrapped-saas-startup-the-story-of-how-damon-chen-turned-failure-into-fuel-for-business-success/)), and his "$1M solo" headline adds his later AI product, PDF.ai ([Behind the Craft](https://creatoreconomy.so/p/damon-chen-engineer-to-one-million)). Jon Yongfook's Bannerbear sat at about $75K MRR in August 2025 ([Supabird](https://supabird.io/articles/jon-yongfook-s-wild-ride-from-corporate-burnout-to-75k-mrr)), and Tim Bennetto's Pallyy reached $74K MRR solo before he began hiring a developer ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/heres-how-i-ve-built-pallyy-to-74k-mrr-solo-a5d9c78766)). Carrd and ProjectionLab show the more common path of solo to the threshold, then a small team. AJ raised about $2M from Rainfall Ventures while Carrd was already profitable ([Fathom](https://usefathom.com/blog/bootstrapped-to-venture-capital)), and ProjectionLab announced $1M ARR "with zero funding" in June 2025 ([ProjectionLab](https://projectionlab.com/blog/we-reached-1m-arr-with-zero-funding)) and was hiring a second engineer by January 2026 ([ProjectionLab](https://cdn.projectionlab.com/blog/were-growing-the-team)).

Elsaid was solo only through Chatbase's first $1M; the company now has 18–30 staff ([ProductLed](https://productled.com/blog/how-chatbase-hit-8m-arr-with-18-people)).

## Most software figures are self-reported, and three questions expose inflation

The evidence behind these names varies far more than the headlines suggest, and ranking the sources makes the differences clear.

| Evidence tier | What qualifies | Cases in this report |
|---|---|---|
| Hard | Public-company deal disclosures, official publisher unit sales, press that inspected the books, revenue verified by the payment processor | Base44 deal; Stardew, Balatro, Papers, Please and Manor Lords sales; Medvi's 2025 revenue; Postiz and Marc Lou's products on TrustMRR; Chatbase's $10M ARR in a Stripe customer story |
| Consistent self-disclosure | The founder's own dated numbers, repeated over years | Perham (2017 to 2023), Levels, Bannerbear's milestone posts, ProjectionLab's blog, Marc Lou's monthly totals, TypingMind's P&L post |
| Single claim | One interview, podcast, X bio or "since launch" banner | Zigpoll, Photopea, Carrd, HeadshotPro, Wave AI, Musicfy, Slopes, Shot Pattern, Flappy Bird |
| Model estimate | Latka/ZoomInfo, Sensor Tower, Appfigures, Gamalytic, revenue inferred from Steam review counts | Widgetsmith, Lethal Company, Schedule I, Vampire Survivors, Geometry Dash |

**Third-party revenue estimates are unreliable for private software companies.** Latka puts Sidekiq at $2M while Perham says he is "closer to 10 million than 1 million" ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/sidekiq)); for Nomad List, Latka shows both its own $704K estimate for 2023 and a ZoomInfo figure of about $5.3M ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/nomad-list)); and its $96K for HeadshotPro is plainly stale ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/headshotpro)). Aggregator profile sites copy each other and founders' X bios, so agreement among them is not independent confirmation. **No private solo business in this set has audited financials**, and none of the classic-era founders turned up with a Stripe-verified TrustMRR page.

Most inflated claims fail one of three questions. The first is whether the figure is ARR, an annualized run-rate or lifetime revenue. fly.pieter.com's "$1M ARR in 17 days" annualizes a few weeks of in-game ad sponsorships ([levels.io](https://levels.io/full-multiplayer-python-websockets-game-ai)), while 404 Media reported about $50,000 a month ([404 Media](https://404media.co/this-game-created-by-ai-vibe-coding-makes-50-000-a-month-yours-probably-wont)); Levels says his Photo AI figure is "Not MRR btw but monthly revenue cause lots of people buy annual plans" ([teardown](https://td-site-dx7.pages.dev/case-studies/photo-ai/)); HeadshotPro's $10M is lifetime; and TypingMind and ShipFast sell one-time licences, so their "ARR" is really annualized revenue. The second question is whether the number covers one product or a portfolio. Testimonial.to and Bannerbear reached $1M only as multi-product totals, and Simon Høiberg's "$1M+ ARR" combines several products while FeedHive alone is estimated at about $228K ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/feedhive)) and has a co-founder ([ThreadReader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1423312746255421451.html)). The third question is whether the founder was solo at the moment of crossing or only at the start. Base44 had 6–8 staff at the sale ([DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/wix-buys-ai-startup-base44-for-80m-six-month-sprint-deal)), TypingMind had about three employees by October 2024 ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/typingmind/team)) even though a 2026 newsletter claims none, and GMass grew to about 11 people ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/gmass)).

Several famous "solo" stories collapse under these checks. BuiltWith is widely repeated as "$14M a year with zero employees", but co-founder Andrew Rogers says the revenue figures were misreported ([Medium](https://medium.com/@andrewjrogers/the-story-of-builtwith-e3bbc17c239f)). Wingify/VWO, often cited as a solo bootstrapped success, was co-founded before it sold for about $200M ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/23/everstone-acquires-bootstrapped-indian-startup-wingify-for-200m)). Baremetrics already had six full-time employees at about $27K MRR ([Baremetrics](https://baremetrics.com/blog/0-to-25000)). Tibo Louis-Lucas, promoted as a solo AI founder, is described elsewhere as running $12M ARR across five products with a team of 10 ([The SaaS Podcast](https://pocketcasts.com/podcast/the-saas-podcast-growing-profitable-ai-saas-ai-agents/772437b0-1d9b-0132-b2f6-5f4c86fd3263/bootstrapped-saas-12m-arr-across-5-products-with-a-team-of-10/60efcaed-0e85-4594-a314-5572c753fa10)). Pckgr's "$1M niche" post itself mentions a "small team" ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/carving-out-a-1m-yr-niche-out-of-the-microsoft-ecosystem-ekxDn4Rdn8zD3uZU2asY)). The creator of 2048 released it free and open source and earned little while clones topped the charts ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2048_(video_game))). One reviewer of the revenue figures in showcases of apps built with Lovable, an AI app builder, warned that they are "four claims relayed together, not four independently verified accounts" ([ProvenStartups](https://provenstartups.com/blog/built-with-ai/apps-built-with-lovable)).

**The biggest mislabel is Medvi.** Its April 2026 New York Times profile prompted a Marginal Revolution post titled "Sam Altman's prediction has come through" ([Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/04/sam-altmans-prediction-has-come-through.html)), referring to Altman's reported bet on when the first one-person billion-dollar company would appear ([Founder Institute](https://fi.co/insight/the-one-person-unicorn-how-solo-founders-are-building-billion-dollar-companies-with-ai-in-2026)). The verified part is real: the NYT reviewed Medvi's financials and reported **$401M in 2025 sales** ([The Star/NYT](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/04/11/how-ai-helped-1-man-and-his-brother-build-a-us18-billion-company)). But Medvi is a GLP-1 telehealth marketer with two full-time employees (the founder and his brother) plus contractors, and it outsources the clinical and pharmacy work to partners ([Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/robotica-e-ia/nyt-publico-historia-emprendedor-ia-que-factura-1-800-millones-dos-empleados-se-olvido-mencionar-unas-cuantas-cosas)). The headline "$1.8B" is projected 2026 revenue, not a valuation ([Investing 101](https://investing101.substack.com/p/the-1b-rorschach-test)), and the FDA had already sent the company a warning letter in February 2026 ([Drug Discovery Trends](https://www.drugdiscoverytrends.com/the-new-york-times-spotlighted-medvi-the-fda-had-already-warned-the-self-proclaimed-fastest-growing-company-in-history/)). As of November 2026, no pure-software solo founder is anywhere near a $1B valuation.

**Verification is improving but stays narrow.** TrustMRR reads revenue directly from Stripe, RevenueCat and other payment providers and refreshes hourly ([ExitBid](https://exitbid.io/blog/trustmrr-review-2026)), but snapshots can go stale (one listing showed $19.3K "verified MRR" next to $0 current MRR ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/solo-founder))). It verifies only revenue that passes through the payment processor, not profit or whether the buyers are genuine customers, and 98% of the revenue it verifies runs through Stripe ([Marc Lou on X](https://x.com/marclou/status/2077736510657626203)). Even a "verified" figure is gross processor revenue from a self-selected population.

## Distribution existed before the product in almost every case

### Launches borrowed an audience or rode a demand spike

**Nearly every solo $1M+ story starts with attention the founder already had or a wave the founder caught.** Levels launched Nomad List as the fourth of his "12 startups in 12 months" ([levels.io](https://levels.io/projects)); it hit #1 on both Product Hunt and Hacker News, bringing about 50,000 visitors from HN and 12,000 from Product Hunt ([levels.io](https://levels.io/product-hunt-hacker-news-number-one)), and Remote OK then launched off the Nomad List audience. AJ already had a following from his free HTML5 UP templates before Carrd's Product Hunt launch in March 2016 ([first-mrr](https://first-mrr.com/study/carrd)). Perham had blogged about advanced Ruby for years, and Goel had already built and sold an email company, JangoMail ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/interview/how-i-hit-115k-mo-with-a-status-quo-improvement-c45d11ad17)). Kutskir's traffic surged after a late-2018 Reddit AMA with about 50,000 upvotes ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-reddit-ama-earned-photopea-12m-monthly-users-7c66c1d9ad)). Chen sold $199 lifetime deals on Product Hunt for about $6K in two weeks ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/testimonial/2-000-revenue-in-7-days--MPL-_yFhZ1AvFa8Ik1X)), and Marc Lou's TrustMRR drew 35,000 visitors in 48 hours from his X audience ([Atticus Li](https://atticusli.com/startup/marc-lou/)).

The clearest exception shows that timing can substitute for audience. Chatbase launched to **16 Twitter followers** ([Solo Founders](https://solofounders.beehiiv.com/p/9m-arr-no-vc-solo-founder)) and reached $1M ARR in 117 days on the ChatGPT wave. The fastest AI-era climbs all launched into a new capability, whether custom chatbots in early 2023, AI voice covers or vibe coding in 2025, which suggests that in the very fast cases timing mattered more than founder skill.

### Scale came from one compounding channel, rarely from paid ads

After launch, growth to $1M came from a channel that strengthens as the product grows. Carrd's "Made with Carrd" footer on every free site is its main distribution loop, and the product claims zero marketing ([Growthmap](https://growthmap-www.pages.dev/blog/carrd-growth-teardown/)). Sidekiq's free library spreads through the Rails ecosystem, and Postiz pairs its self-hostable GitHub repo with a 30% affiliate program ([Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/postiz)). Zigpoll credits the Shopify App Store with anywhere from a third to 90% of its business, depending on the source ([Niche Pursuits](https://www.nichepursuits.com/?p=142837)), and GMass stayed free inside Gmail for about its first year before charging ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/interview/how-i-hit-115k-mo-with-a-status-quo-improvement-c45d11ad17)) on its way to about 1M signups. HeadshotPro's affiliate program reportedly brings in more than $50K a month ([Supabird](https://supabird.io/articles/danny-postma-how-a-solo-hacker-built-an-ai-empire-from-bali)). For consumer apps and games the compounding channel was social video and streamers, as with Widgetsmith on TikTok and Lethal Company on Twitch and YouTube ([esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/gaming/lethal-company-the-sleeper-hit-horror-game-that-is-taking-over-twitch-and-youtube)), or subscription-service deals, as with Manor Lords launching day one on Xbox Game Pass ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/pc-indie-darling-manor-lords-hits-2-million-copies-sold-three-weeks-after-huge-launch-on-steam-game-pass)) and Brotato drawing more than 1M Game Pass installs ([Pure Xbox](https://purexbox.com/news/2024/02/xbox-game-pass-indie-brotato-proves-popular-on-microsofts-subscription-service)).

**Paid acquisition was rarely decisive.** Goel says GMass doubled revenue after he stopped spending thousands on Facebook ads ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/gmass-inc-b9e9cf9c-98b3-418b-9b1b-b4456ce3070f)). The paid influencer and user-generated-video engine behind the 2024–2026 mobile hits belongs to teams, such as one running about 50 TikTok accounts for about $200K MRR ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/50-tiktok-accounts-ai-200-000-mrr-for-mobile-app-bd8f6f48ac)), and no verified solo iOS developer reached $100K MRR that way. When solo founders did grind out content marketing deliberately, it worked slowly: Yongfook's year of content and SEO took Bannerbear from $10K to $27K MRR ([Bannerbear](https://bannerbear.com/blog/one-year-of-marketing-a-saas-from-10k-to-20k-mrr)).

### Classic builders needed four to nine years; AI cut that to months

The classic timelines match the broader data. ChartMogul finds that the median bootstrapped SaaS company takes about 3.5 years to reach $1M ARR and the top quartile about two years, only about four months behind VC-backed peers ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-vc-bootstrapped/)). Many classic founders kept their day jobs for much of that stretch: Nolan quit only when ProjectionLab reached about $200K ARR ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/kyle-nolan-finally-turned-his-side-hustle-into-his-main-hustle-at-200k-arr-but-kept-his-job-84whHWnbr6KiqdNbM8uf)), and Williams ran Designjoy on the side for 3.5 years ([Indie Hackers podcast](https://chartable.com/podcasts/the-indie-hackers-podcast/episodes/110556717-249-the-keys-to-making-1myear-as-a-solo-founder-with-brett-williams-of-designjoy)). The AI compression shows up across startups of every size: top AI companies reached $1M in annualized revenue in a median of about 11 months against about 15 for SaaS ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-hu/blog/inside-the-growth-of-the-top-ai-companies-on-stripe)), and 20% of startups incorporated through Stripe Atlas charged their first customer within 30 days in 2025, up from 8% in 2020 ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/annual-updates/2025)).

## Reaching $1M is a top-1% outcome, and AI widened the gap

**The base rate is the single most important fact for an aspiring builder, and the indie-hacker feed hides it.** RevenueCat's 2026 report, covering more than 115,000 subscription apps, finds that only **4.6% of newly launched apps reach $10,000 in monthly revenue within two years**; the top 10% grew more than 306% while median year-over-year growth was 5.3% ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). Secondary summaries of the same report add that 57.7% of new apps never earn $1,000 in total and the top 10% capture 94.5% of revenue ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/)). On TrustMRR, **1.0% of listed startups sit in the $1M+ band** ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/stats)), although the page does not say whether that band measures annual or lifetime revenue; a scrape of about 12,900 listings found just 12 startups (0.1%) at $250K+ MRR ([Crawlora](https://crawlora.net/datasets/trustmrr)), and median MRR across tracked startups is $334 ([Quasa](https://quasa.io/media/startup-reality-the-hard-numbers-from-trustmrr)). One builder's published four-year ledger shows 26 projects, $115K in total revenue and **18 projects that made exactly $0** ([buildmvpfast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/indie-hacking-real-numbers-4-years-26-projects-115k-2026)). Because founders choose whether to list on TrustMRR, its population already skews toward success, so the true share of new apps that reach $1M ARR is plausibly around 1% or lower. That is an inference; no dataset measures it directly.

**AI produced more winners at the top and a weaker middle.** Stripe Economics counts more than twice as many solopreneurs earning over $1M in 2025 as in 2023, and close to three times as many crossing $5M and $10M ([Stripe Economics](https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-age-of-the-solopreneur)). Solo founding exploded over the same period: Carta's solo-founder share of new startups rose from 23.7% in 2019 to 36.3% in the first half of 2025 ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/solo-founders-report)), and Stripe Atlas reports that solo founders formed 63% of its new C-corps in Q2 2026 ([IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/ai-agents-help-entrepreneurs-run-businesses-alone-3809273)), although Atlas classifies founders as solo by self-reported headcount at signup. The middle weakened as competition rose: one analysis of Stripe data finds that the median solo Atlas startup's first-six-month revenue fell 23% in 2025 while the top decile rose 19% ([Earning Living Online](https://www.earninglivingonline.com/stripe-solopreneur-revenue-gap-top-median-2026/)), though another summary covering all founders reports a 39% median rise ([buildmvpfast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/stripe-2025-annual-letter-key-takeaways)). The data also cuts against the "solo is optimal" story: in MicroConf's 2024 survey of bootstrapped SaaS founders, three-founder teams grow 2–3× faster and full-time founders grow 2.2× faster than part-timers ([MicroConf](https://x.com/MicroConf/status/1811478998217634242)).

**Durability is the AI cohort's weak point.** ShipFast fell from about $126K a month at its 2024 peak to about $2K in 2026 ([aiidelist](https://aiidelist.com/blog/shipfast-revenue-collapse-marc-lou-2026)), Marc Lou's 2025 total was 20% below 2024's, fly.pieter.com's revenue reportedly reached $0 ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/promptway/he-built-a-flight-simulator-in-three-hours-and-hit-1m-a-year-in-17-days-then-it-went-to-zero-1b1l)), and Photo AI slid from about $138K to about $100K a month. The products that held up moved upmarket, built an open-source moat, repositioned for AI agents or sold near the peak. TypingMind leaned on team plans; Chatbase now positions itself against well-funded rivals Sierra, Decagon and Intercom's Fin ([WBOC](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/press_releases/chatbase-reaches-10m-arr-as-a-profitable-challenger-to-sierra-decagon-and-fin/article_ea903107-b04c-55a9-a12d-810797b1f35b.html)); Postiz combined an open-source community with an agent repositioning; and Base44 sold. Yongfook, who rebuilt Bannerbear with AI coding agents, puts it bluntly: "competing on features is over, because anyone can build any feature in a weekend now" ([Bannerbear](https://www.bannerbear.com/blog/how-to-pivot-your-saas-company-in-the-age-of-ai/)). One founder's Codex-related app reached about $9K a month before OpenAI shipped overlapping features ([Steal What Works](https://stealwhatworks.com/blogs/news/indie-hacking-too-crowded)). The sources document these declines but not their causes, so blaming foundation-model competition for any specific drop remains an inference.

**Dependence on a single platform is the most common way a solo business dies suddenly.** Reddit's new API price of $0.24 per 1,000 calls would have cost Apollo about $20M a year against revenue of roughly $500K, and Selig shut the app down on 30 June 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/08/popular-third-party-reddit-app-apollo-is-shutting-down-as-a-result-of-reddits-new-api-pricing/)). Google's September 2023 Helpful Content Update cut the independent review site HouseFresh's Google traffic by about 95% ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/housefresh-achieves-notable-traffic-recovery-after-google-algorithm-impacts-2/)). A Shopify policy change restricted CartHook and a Meta API shutdown hurt PostMyParty ([Micro SaaS Bytes](https://microsaasbytes.substack.com/p/platform-risks-for-indie-hackers)). Outside shocks cut both ways: COVID gave Plague Inc. "a year's worth of revenue … and then it went to nothing" ([Mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/what-happens-to-a-game-about-pandemics-when-theres-an-actual-pandemic/)), and Sony and Warner sent Musicfy copyright claims ([Partnerkin](https://partnerkin.com/en/blog/case_study/arib-khan-musicfy)).

**Churn quietly caps how big a subscription business can get.** Jason Cohen's "Max MRR" frames it simply: at a constant churn rate, the dollars lost each month grow with the base, so growth flattens near a ceiling set by new revenue divided by churn ([A Smart Bear](https://longform.asmartbear.com/max-mrr/)). In customer terms, 100 new customers a month at 5% monthly churn caps out at 2,000 customers ([ChartMogul](https://blog.chartmogul.com/saas-growth-ceiling/)), and a $32K-MRR product with 9% monthly churn has a ceiling near $40K ([Smart Bear Live](https://www.podengine.ai/podcasts/smart-bear-live)). Applied to $1M ARR, roughly $83K MRR, simple arithmetic shows a founder at 5% monthly churn must add about $4.2K of new MRR every month just to stay level, against about $1.7K at 2% churn. That gap explains why low-churn B2B and developer tools, ad-funded free tools and one-time-purchase games reach $1M more readily than cheap consumer subscriptions.

## Low-support models stay solo; the rest hire or sell

**Whether a founder stays solo past $1M depends mostly on how much support each dollar of revenue creates.** The builders who stayed strictly or nearly solo run community memberships and job posts (Levels), self-serve licences (Perham), ads (Kutskir), app-store SaaS (Zigelbaum), digital products at about $4K a month in costs (Marc Lou ([yespress](https://yespress.io/marc-lou))), premium games (Barone) and consumer subscriptions (Smith, Mohrer). The builders who hired mostly sell products that need support or live operations. GMass grew to about 11 people, Carrd raised money and hired, Chatbase grew to 18–30 staff, TVGS became "an actual game studio now with an office" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/life-sim/schedule-1-developer-tvgs-is-an-actual-game-studio-now-with-an-office-desks-and-a-new-guy-named-rob-by-the-end-of-the-year-there-will-likely-be-4-people-working-on-schedule-1)), and Vampire Survivors' studio poncle now runs 15+ projects across studios in the UK, Italy and Japan ([The Nerd Stash](https://thenerdstash.com/vampire-survivors-developer-goes-wild-with-over-15-new-games-expands-to-japan-and-italy-profit-is-not-a-priority/)). Carta finds that solo founders make their first hire at a median of **399 days**, sooner than multi-founder companies at 480 ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/solo-founders-report)), so solo is often a starting state rather than an end state. AI tools are the newest way to delay hiring: Mohrer runs Wave with Claude Code, and Levels titled a May 2026 post "I don't write code anymore" ([levels.io](https://levels.io/llms.txt)). The evidence that this keeps a subscription app one-person at multi-million ARR still rests mostly on Mohrer's self-reports. The human cost is real even in the best outcome: asked about loneliness, Shlomo said "Yes, absolutely I felt lonely" ([Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/rkogikc9le)).

**Exits for small solo businesses are priced on profit, and an owner the business depends on pulls the price down.** The median SaaS sale on Acquire.com was about **3.9× annual profit**, with most deals between 1× and 6× and profitable listings averaging a 71% margin ([Acquire.com](https://blog.acquire.com/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-jan-2026/)). Flippa's average was about 2.47× profit in the first half of 2026 ([Acquire.com](https://blog.acquire.com/business-valuation-multiples-by-industry/)), while brokers cite about 4–6× ARR for clean, profitable businesses between $1M and $5M ARR ([Livmo](https://livmo.com/blog/saas-valuation-multiples-2026/)). By arithmetic, a solo $1M-ARR SaaS at a 70% margin is worth roughly $2.7M at Acquire's median profit multiple, or $4–6M at broker ARR multiples; the gap reflects who the buyer is and how much the business depends on its owner. Actual outcomes span four orders of magnitude.

| Exit | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wordle to the New York Times | "Low seven figures" ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2022/01/the-new-york-times-buys-wordle-for-a-price-in-the-low-seven-figures/)) | No prior revenue |
| Baremetrics | $4M cash, about 2.65× ARR ([Baremetrics](https://baremetrics.com/blog/i-sold-baremetrics)) | Not solo at $1M |
| Base44 to Wix | $80M up front | Up to about $280M in total |
| Minecraft to Microsoft | $2.5B | Solo phase ended in 2010 |

Selling carries costs beyond the multiple. Tibo sold two products for about $8M and says he would never do it again ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/heres-how-tibo-sold-two-products-for-8m-and-why-he-ll-never-do-it-again-iwykhfENoZqJgRLU33zv)). Users of the Mac menu-bar utility Bartender, which holds Accessibility and Screen Recording permissions, learned of its quiet sale only afterwards ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/06/04/bartender-mac-app-new-owner/)), and Nova Launcher's new owner, Branch, cut the team to one person and did not honour a promise to open-source the app ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/nova-launcher-shutdown-3595611/)).

**The educators' playbooks line up closely with these cases.** Rob Walling's "stair-step" advises against building a standalone SaaS first: start with a small product sold through a single channel, repeat until the income replaces your salary, then build the SaaS ([MicroConf](https://microconf.com/latest/the-stairstep-approach-to-bootstrapping)). Walling also stresses that most bootstrappers underprice and that churn must be fixed before marketing is scaled ([Founder Casebook](https://foundercasebook.com/guides/rob-walling-microconf-bootstrapped-saas)). Levels' 12 startups in 12 months ([levels.io](https://levels.io/12-startups-12-months)) and Daniel Vassallo's portfolio of small bets ([Small Bets](https://smallbets.com/)) put the same idea into practice: run many cheap experiments and kill the losers quickly. Publishing verified revenue now works as marketing in its own right: TrustMRR, itself a solo product, reached 4 million visitors, 208 startups acquired through its marketplace and **$427,610 in revenue at a 90% margin** in its first year ([Marc Lou on X](https://x.com/marclou/status/2116155913904259373)). And MicroConf finds that targeting enterprise customers correlates with the fastest monthly growth, about 27% ([MicroConf](https://microconf.com/founders/1m-arr)), which fits the pattern of the durable solo businesses above.

## Conclusion

The record points to a shift in what is scarce. When building was expensive, the solo founders who reached $1M won by being strong engineers in niches others ignored: Perham, Kutskir and Barone each spent years on products few people could have built alone. AI has made building cheap (Levels shipped a flight simulator in hours, and Yongfook says features no longer differentiate), so the scarce inputs are now distribution and durability. The solo winners of 2023–2026 bear this out, because the ones who lasted owned an audience, an open-source community or a B2B customer base rather than just code. Tibo's line that distribution is the moat when AI makes building cheap ([SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/482)) reads less like a slogan than a description of who survived.

For an aspiring builder, the realistic model is not Base44 but a narrow, low-churn product with one compounding channel, a business model light enough on support for one person to run, and a plan measured in years. Fast AI-era revenue is best treated as a window: convert it into recurring B2B revenue or a defensible moat, or sell before the feature is commoditized, as Base44 did and ShipFast did not. Anyone presenting a solo $1M story, including their own, should be ready to say whether the figure is ARR or lifetime revenue, one product or a portfolio, and solo at the moment of crossing. Four questions remain open: no dataset isolates how long solo founders take to reach $1M, no churn data exists for the AI-era products, no verified solo $1M+ software founder turned up in India or Latin America, and the claim that AI coding agents can keep a multi-million-dollar subscription business truly one-person rests largely on a single self-reported case, Wave AI.
