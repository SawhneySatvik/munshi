Munshi site options, round 2

Munshi's own product website · Thursday 29 October 2026 · nothing is built until you pick

Round 1 gave you four colourings of one page. This round changes the page itself. Each of the six options has its own hero, its own way of showing the product, its own section rhythm and navigation, its own palette and its own type. None of them uses indigo or saffron.

Every preview is live HTML. Tap the underlined figures to open their records, press Approve or Reject, and use Reset demo to start again. Desktop is rendered at 1280 px and scaled to fit; the phone is 375 px and scrolls inside. Every shop, figure and supplier in the previews is synthetic, and no prices are shown because none are decided.

References studied

  • Stripe shows the product doing its job in the first viewport, at a size you can read, with believable sample data. The detail is the proof.
  • Linear restraint: few colours, one type family, and motion only where a state changes.
  • Mercury calm money. Generous space, muted colour and no hype read as trust.
  • Wise shows the arithmetic. Fees broken into lines anyone can check make transparency the hero.
  • Ramp one confident accent on a disciplined neutral system; product crops cut tight to the one detail that matters.
  • Zerodha plain words on plain pages. Saying what the product will not do earns more trust than saying what it will.
  • Khatabook Hindi first, phone first, and the ledger owners already keep as the mental model.
  • Square lets the visitor pick their kind of business, then talks about that business.

Principles only. No layout, copy, logo or asset was taken from any of them. Each option names the ones it drew on.

My recommendation

Recommended site-E · Statement. It proves both promises in the first screen, using a thing shop owners already trust: a day book where every figure opens its record and the pending line waits for approval. It stays calm, works as a list on a phone, and its petrol and rose sit well away from round 1. Risk: it is the densest hero, so the headline has to stay short.

Runner-up site-J · Shutter. The most memorable of the six, and the only one where the three kinds of shop are the front door. Risk: the street can tip into costume if the illustration is pushed further. It needs restraint.

If you want the safe choice, site-I is the category standard done well. If you want Hindi to lead, take site-H.

Statement

Recommended

The page is a day book. The hero is a statement of today's entries: every figure unfolds its record under the line, and one pending line waits for your approval before it posts.

Ink on paper 14.9:1 · muted on paper 6.0:1 · cover text on petrol 9.5:1 · cover muted on petrol 5.9:1 · rose on paper 5.8:1 · rose ink on wash 6.2:1

Type

Books you can check, line by line.

हर अंक के पीछे उसकी पर्ची।

Biryani for everything: one family drawn for Latin and Devanagari together. 800 display, 400 body, 600 figures in tabular lining numerals.

Design choices

  • Navigation: a two-tier header on a petrol cover: a thin utility row holding Log in, and a main row holding Sign up.
  • Product: no phone mock-up. The day book is the product; tap a figure and its record unfolds beneath its line (the bill, the count, the sum).
  • How it works: the statement's own notes, lettered a, b, c to match the marks in the table. The shop types are three mini statements.
  • Radius, borders, shadow: 4–6px on the sheet, pills for buttons, hairline rules between rows only, one soft shadow under the sheet. No outlines.
  • Density: tabular and calm. Rose appears only on the line waiting for you.
  • Motion: a record unfolds from its row (clip reveal, 320 ms); on approval the rose row turns into an ordinary line and the closing balance re-inks.

References studied: Wise (show the arithmetic line by line), Khatabook (the ledger owners already keep), Zerodha (plain words, nothing hidden).

Type

Ask your shop. Check every answer.

आज कितना उधार वापस आया?

Gabarito for Latin display and UI; Akshar for Devanagari, including the spoken question set large.

Design choices

  • Navigation: a floating pill (wordmark, three links, Log in, Sign up) that stays on top as you scroll; no full-width bar.
  • Product: the thread itself, with no device frame: question, answer, draft. The record sheet sits open beside it (below it on a phone).
  • How it works: "Two rules Munshi never breaks", each with a small live-looking strip, including a Cannot verify line.
  • Radius, borders, shadow: 18–20px on sheets, pill buttons, 1px warm hairlines; depth from tone steps, one long shadow under the record sheet.
  • Density: low. A narrow reading column and one idea per screen; the dark suits the owner checking the day after closing.
  • Motion: the record sheet slides in 14px and fades; approving folds the draft into a single confirmed line.

References studied: Linear (dark restraint, motion only where a state changes), Khatabook (Hindi first, voice-friendly), Mercury (calm, no hype).

Type

Nothing leaves your shop without your signature.

आपके दस्तख़त के बिना कुछ नहीं बदलता।

Brygada 1918 for Latin display and body (serif body at 17–18px); Noto Serif Devanagari for Hindi.

Design choices

  • Navigation: a letterhead: the wordmark centred, links on the left, Log in and Sign up on the right, a double brass rule beneath.
  • Product: a centred headline over one wide draft leaf: counterfoil on the left, a perforation, the leaf on a fine drawn guilloche ground.
  • Signature moment: tap a figure and the counterfoil shows its record; press Approve and the signature draws itself (1.1 s stroke).
  • Rhythm: two alternating rows with drawn illustrations, then a "Made for a kirana, a salon or a suit shop" sentence you can switch.
  • Radius, borders, shadow: 6px on the leaf, pills for buttons, 1px green at 30%, one long soft shadow under the leaf.
  • Density: generous and letterhead-formal, the calmest of the light options.

References studied: Mercury (calm money, restraint as trust), Stripe (precise product detail at a size you can read).

Pakka

The page is a bilingual board: Hindi set huge above English, one giant figure that opens its bills, and one colour on the whole page, which appears only when you approve.

  • Rule#E2E2E2Light rules

Ink on white 19.7:1 · muted on white 6.7:1 · white on band 19.7:1 · muted on band 9.5:1 · white on green 5.8:1

Type

Books you can check.

पक्का हिसाब।

Archivo with its width axis (78–100%) for Latin; Khand for Devanagari display. Hindi always sits above English.

Design choices

  • Navigation: a left rail on desktop (wordmark, links, Log in, Sign up); a plain top bar on a phone.
  • Hero: a poster: पक्का हिसाब set at nearly 200px, English beneath, stacked like a station name board.
  • Product: one figure at 168px. Tapping it drops a black band listing the bills behind it.
  • Approval: a ruled row; approving wipes it green, the first colour you see on the page.
  • Radius, borders, shadow: none, none, none. 1px rules, square buttons; scale contrast does all the hierarchy.
  • Motion: the band wipes down (clip-path, 380 ms); the green fills left to right on approval (560 ms).

References studied: Zerodha (white page, plain promises), Ramp (one accent, rationed), Linear (scale instead of decoration).

Type

Your shop's books, with every figure on the record.

हर आँकड़ा अपने रिकॉर्ड के साथ।

Onest for Latin; Noto Sans Devanagari for Hindi, the steadiest Devanagari for UI text.

Design choices

  • Navigation: a standard top bar on the dark hero; Sign up is the lime button.
  • Product: the full workspace held inside the dark hero, framed like a screen: sidebar, four figures, a record pane, a drafts strip.
  • Interaction and motion: select a figure and the record pane shows its bills (260 ms rise); Margin today shows Cannot verify with its reason; Approve tints the draft lime over 400 ms.
  • Rhythm: two tight product crops, then a Kirana / Salon / Suit shop switch, then a dark CTA band.
  • Radius, borders, shadow: 16px on the workspace, 9–12px on controls, 1px cool rules, one deep shadow under the workspace only.
  • Density: the highest of the six. It shows the most product and is the safest choice.

References studied: Stripe (the product in the first viewport), Ramp (one accent on a disciplined neutral, tight crops), Square (pick your kind of business).

Type

Raise the shutter. The books are already open.

शटर उठाइए, हिसाब तैयार है।

Kadwa for signboards and headlines in both scripts (a sturdy painted-sign serif); Sarala for body text in both scripts.

Design choices

  • Navigation: a jamun signboard band across the top; Log in as text, Sign up as a blush plaque.
  • Hero: the three shop types are the front door: three shopfronts with painted signs, one shutter up on today's books.
  • Interaction: raising a shutter is the shop switch; pista marks the edge that moved, the page's only bright colour.
  • How it works: "Behind the shutter": tap a figure to open its record card; a draft waits for Approve. The footer is the shutter coming down.
  • Radius, borders, shadow: 6–10px, 1px blush and rose rules, shutter ribs drawn flat in CSS, a small shadow only under the rolled drum.
  • Motion: the shutter rolls up in 720 ms with an ease-out; with reduced motion it switches at once.

References studied: Square (start from the kind of business), Mercury (calm pacing between dense moments).
