# Owner home: two visual directions

Both pages carry the same content: Sharma General Store (synthetic), the three-item morning brief, the reorder draft, the
voice button and one "Cannot verify" line. Both work on a phone, so open them there and try them. The EN/हिंदी toggle
switches the language. Approve and Reject each show their result, and "Reset mock" puts the draft back. Tapping any number,
or its source chip, opens the source it was read from. They are plain HTML, CSS and drawn SVG, using two Google Fonts per
page. All data is synthetic and the supplier is simulated.

**A · Today's sheet (`a/index.html`).** This one is built on the calendar every wholesaler hands a kirana, the one that
hangs behind the counter. Every figure is set like a date on that calendar: a big numeral in a ruled cell (Teko), with its
source printed small in the cell's corner, the way the calendar prints the tithi. It uses two inks on white: black for the
reference figure and vermilion for the figure that needs you, the way a calendar uses red for its Sundays. Headings always
show Hindi and English side by side, like the calendar's month line. Approving circles the order quantity in red pen, the
way an owner rings a date. The voice button is a red slab that stays fixed at the bottom. This direction is strongest at a
glance. The numbers are large and calm, and the screen reads well in street daylight. It leads with the brief and then the
decision, and on a phone all three findings and the Approve button fit above the fold. Its risk is that the calendar
reference is subtle: someone who misses it sees a clean, sober red-and-black app, which may be no bad thing.

**B · The slip (`b/index.html`).** This one is built on the distributor's carbon-copy bill book. The ground is the yellow
triplicate sheet. Each finding is a white slip of bill-style rows: what the figure is, the amount, and a red serial in
numbering-machine type naming the record it came from. The draft is the pink duplicate, the copy that would go out to the
supplier. Violet appears only where a named person acts. The Approve button is violet, and approving lands a rubber stamp
on the slip: "APPROVED · स्वीकृत · RAMESH SHARMA", in rough ink with a short thump. Rejecting lands a "REJECTED" stamp the
same way. The page leads with the decision (the slip waiting for your stamp) and puts the morning's slips under it as
evidence. The language toggle switches the whole page, rather than showing both languages at once. This direction is
more tactile and more tabular, with a label, an amount and a serial on every row, and it makes the guarantee easy to see: every number has a serial, and nothing moves without a
person's stamp. Its risks are that it is the more expected kirana metaphor, so it could tip into costume if pushed
further, and that on a phone only the first one and a half findings show above the fold, because the decision comes first.
